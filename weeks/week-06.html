Week 6 Recap (2026-04-27 — 2026-05-03)

Monday, May 4, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

After Monday's results, Eephus had All Betts Are Off pinned 9-2-1 — nine categories to two — and this one looked cooked. Seven days later it ended 9-3-0, in the complete opposite direction, with three lead changes and a comeback from a seven-category hole. The difference came in the rotation: seven quality starts, a 1.93 ERA, 55 strikeouts. Eephus posted a pristine 0.93 WHIP across 39.2 innings but managed only two quality starts, and when the All Betts rotation started banking QS after QS, the categories followed in bulk.

Across the bracket, Ep-Skenes Files survived Jr Jays by a 6-5-1 final with four lead changes and an AVG margin of .003. Jr Jays ran up 41 runs and a .369 OBP and walked out the loser, which tells you what a 4.79 ERA costs in this format. Ep-Skenes won ERA going away (3.50 to 4.79), clipped the AVG by a thread, and held on through a final two days of lead swaps. This league rewards pitching depth — when batting splits run close, ERA breaks the tie.

Juiced Balls started the week down five categories to Cease and Desist, trailing 7-2-3 after Monday. By Tuesday the lead had flipped, and it never flipped back. The reason: a 2.02 ERA, a 1.03 WHIP, a K/BB ratio of 8.00, six quality starts, 72 strikeouts. Cease and Desist hit .319 with 11 home runs and 42 RBI and took five of the six batting categories, surrendering only stolen bases. But a 4.44 ERA gives back seven categories over seven days, and that math doesn't balance. Final: 7-5-0.

The commissioner's office had a good week. Professor McGonigle rolled Kirby's Dream Team 9-2-1, posting a .306 average, a 1.67 ERA, a 0.93 WHIP, 43 RBI, and seven saves and holds. Kirby's Dream Team pitched 32 innings — just over the 30-IP floor — won the runs category by two, and claimed quality starts. A 2-9-1 line from the two-time champion. The Hammer expects better.

Risky Bettiness beat Babe Ruthless 6-5-1 in a week where neither team distinguished themselves on the mound — 4.46 ERA for Risky, 4.28 for Babe — and the whole thing came down to WHIP: 1.58 versus 1.57. One hundredth of a point. Risky Bettiness brought the bats, posting a .404 OBP with 11 home runs and 41 runs. Babe Ruthless countered with strikeouts, ERA, and five saves and holds. When it gets that narrow, both teams did most things right — one side just edged the other in the most microscopic way imaginable.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jordan Walker came into 2026 with something to prove. The Cardinals outfielder was one of the most hyped prospects of his draft class before the big leagues had their way with him — early-career struggles, the classic adjustment period that humbles even the best young talent. This week, he hit .393 with 11 RBI, 2 HR, 2 stolen bases, and posted a .419 OBP, carrying Kirby's Dream Team on his back like a man who finally remembered why everyone was so excited about him coming up through St. Louis. The RBI total alone would win some matchup weeks. The full package — average, power, speed, on-base — is exactly what scouts fell in love with when Walker was a prospect, and this was the week it all showed up on the same invoice.

The runners-up read like a Cardinals-Yankees reunion. Alec Burleson (.407 AVG, .484 OBP, 8 R, 11 RBI) turned in a monster week for Professor McGonigle — if Walker doesn't hit .393, Burleson wins this award without debate. Aaron Judge (.429 AVG, .556 OBP, 3 HR, 8 R) reminded everyone why Risky Bettiness paid through the nose for him at auction; that OBP is a category unto itself. And Ben Rice (3 HR, .409 AVG, .480 OBP) put together a week that had Babe Ruthless grinning — a catcher posting near-.480 OBP while going deep three times is genuinely hard to find, and they found it.

On the mound, Chase Burns earned Pitcher of the Week for All Betts Are Off with a two-start line that showcases exactly what Cincinnati thought they were drafting: 13 IP, 16 K, 1.38 ERA, 0.92 WHIP, and a K/BB of 8. That last number is what separates Burns from runner-up Jesús Luzardo, who actually posted a lower ERA (1.35) and WHIP (0.75) with 18 strikeouts across two starts for Juiced Balls — a week good enough to win this award most other weeks. Burns walked almost nobody, and command like that doesn't come from a hot streak. Special mention to Emerson Hancock, who went 7 innings with 14 punchouts for Juiced Balls — two strikeouts per inning is the kind of line that has managers frantically texting each other on a Tuesday night asking why he's still available.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS — The class of this league, and the distance between them and the field is growing by the week.

#1 All Betts Are Off — 49-22-1 (.688), W 9-3. All Betts Are Off is playing a different game than everyone else in this league right now. A .688 winning percentage at this stage of the season puts the leader in rarified air, and a 9-3 shellacking this week was just another Tuesday for the runaway favorite.

#2 Professor McGonigle — 40-28-4 (.583), W 9-2. Professor McGonigle turned in a dominant 9-2 performance and is still 11 games back in the win column. Second place in this league is a fine address, but the gap to first is becoming a conversation.

#3 Babe Ruthless — 38-32-2 (.542), L 5-6. The defending champion dropped a winnable matchup and slides to third. The record still screams playoff team, but the reigning champ can't afford to hand wins away.

SOLID — Talented enough to beat anyone, inconsistent enough to lose to anyone.

#4 Juiced Balls — 34-35-3 (.493), W 7-5. Juiced Balls clawed out a 7-5 win and crossed back above .500. It's a start. They've been a .500 team trying to become a .550 team all season, and the window for that transformation is closing.

#5 Kirby's Dream Team — 39-30-3 (.563), L 2-9. The best record in this tier got turned in a 2-9 disaster, and that's going to drop the Power Rankings stock hard. The season-long numbers say contender; this week's performance said otherwise. One brutal week doesn't erase a .563 record, but it opens the door to doubt.

MEDIOCRE — Somewhere between "building something" and "running out of runway."

#6 Ep-Skenes Files — 30-37-5 (.451), W 6-5. Ep-Skenes Files squeezed out a 6-5 win and deserves credit for staying relevant. But 30-37 is the ceiling until proven otherwise, and a single-game margin of victory once a week only delays the reckoning.

#7 Jr Jays — 30-37-5 (.451), L 5-6. Jr Jays and Ep-Skenes Files share an identical record and the same tier — the difference this week is Ep-Skenes won and Jr Jays didn't. When the gap between sixth and seventh place is a coin flip, that's not a good place for either of them.

#8 Risky Bettiness — 27-41-4 (.403), W 6-5. Risky Bettiness wins and still owns the league's worst record. A victory matters; the season total matters more. The math on a playoff run from .403 requires a calculator and a miracle.

REBUILDING — The wild card picture is not their problem to solve.

#9 Cease and Desist — 28-41-3 (.410), L 5-7. Cease and Desist keeps losing ground, and the trade deadline conversation is the most relevant thing happening around them right now. Selling in August starts to make more sense with every loss.

#10 Eephus — 29-41-2 (.417), L 3-9. Eephus took a 3-9 beating and lands at the bottom of the board despite technically owning a marginally better record than ninth place. Getting blown out by six categories wipes out any benefit of the doubt. First-year learning curve or not, there's work to do.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Sean Burke spent six innings making the opposition look overmatched, and Ep-Skenes Files looked smart for streaming him. Eight strikeouts, zero runs, an 0.83 WHIP and a quality start — Burke had command of the zone from the first pitch. The 8-to-1 K/BB ratio tells the real story: a starter who walks one and fans eight is pitching from a position of total control, and that was Burke all night. The White Sox rotation doesn't generate much buzz around the league, but Burke has the arm to put up lines like this, and Ep-Skenes Files went and found him.

Clay Holmes makes a strong case for runner-up — 6.2 innings, 1.35 ERA and a quality start for Jr Jays, which is notable given Holmes reinvented himself as a starter after years of closing games for the Yankees. Bryan Baker chipped in a hold for Eephus out of the Tampa pen. The week's best stream belonged to Burke, and it was not particularly close. Find 'em early, ride 'em hard.

The Insider Report

by "Deep Source" DiNapoli

Sources are lighting up my phone this week, and the name I keep hearing is Kirby's Dream Team.

Here's what I'm told: the front office in Kirby-land has been flooding the league with aggressive trade offers, targeting nothing short of the biggest names on the market — Juan Soto and Corbin Carroll. We're not talking modest packages. Sources describe offers involving up to half the roster. A manager in the know called it "very suspicious," and I'll tell you, when a team starts swinging that hard, it means one thing: they've looked at the standings and decided waiting is a losing strategy.

I'm hearing the pitch centers on their rotation depth. Kirby's Dream Team is sitting on a staff that includes George Kirby and Logan Gilbert — two legitimate aces — which gives them real currency. The question every trade partner has to ask themselves is whether consolidating that pitching depth into one franchise bat makes the team better. At 39-30-3 and sitting third in the standings, this is a squad with the record to back an aggressive push. They're nine games over .500. The calendar is still early enough that a Soto or Carroll could change the entire complexion of their batting categories.

The teams on the other side of these offers are worth watching closely. Ep-Skenes Files and Jr Jays are both stalled at .451, and I'm told neither front office has publicly committed to a direction yet. If you're sitting at sixth or seventh with a pitching asset worth coveting, now is the moment to extract maximum value — before the contenders figure out other options. A team like Risky Bettiness at .403 should be listening to every call. The deadline is August 6th. The window to rebuild through trades doesn't stay open forever.

One more thing I can't confirm just yet, but I'm keeping an eye on it: sources suggest at least one other contender — not Kirby's Dream Team — has also been nosing around the Soto market. If multiple teams are targeting the same players simultaneously, that's an auction, and someone is going to overpay. DiNapoli out.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Juiced Balls lost five of the six batting categories this week to Cease and Desist, whose .319 average and 72 hits were the best in the league, and still won the matchup 7-5 by sweeping all six pitching categories — anchored by a K/BB ratio of 8.00 against a league average of 3.34. At more than double the average, that number turns a pitching staff into a category-winning machine regardless of what the offense is doing.

Cease and Desist's week deserves a closer look. They posted the best batting line in the league: .319/.386, 72 hits, 42 RBI, 11 home runs, winning five of the six batting categories. They still lost. A 4.44 ERA surrendered the entire pitching column, and zero stolen bases gave away the only batting category they didn't win. In a 12-category format, giving away that many categories outright is a structural problem.

Risky Bettiness posted the league's best OBP this week at .404, 48 points above the league average. They also posted the league's worst WHIP at 1.58, 35 points above the league average. Their hitters reached base at a rate that outpaced every team in the league. Their pitchers allowed baserunners at a rate that ranked last. Two extreme performances from the same roster, depending entirely on which side of the diamond you watched.

Professor McGonigle's staff threw 37.2 innings — second fewest in the league — with only two quality starts. Their ERA was 1.67, best in the league. Their WHIP was 0.93. They led the league with seven saves-plus-holds. This is a deliberate construction: fewer innings, higher-leverage arms. The record is 9-2-1. The data endorses the approach.
