Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

There are wins you earn and wins you steal at the last second, and All Betts Are Off needed Sunday to make this one official. They led out of the gate, got overtaken mid-week, and watched Juiced Balls build up to a 4-category lead by Thursday. Juiced Balls finished this week with a 0.88 WHIP and a 5.09 K/BB — numbers that, in any reasonable universe, mean you're holding a trophy. But heading into the final day down a single category, All Betts Are Off swept enough stats to flip the board, closing out a 7-5-0 victory with a 5.92 ERA somehow still attached to their name. The bat carried them: 36 runs, six home runs, a .260 average, and a QS edge of 4 to 3. Juiced Balls was better on the mound. They still lost. File that under "it happens."

The Jr Jays comeback is going in the season-end retrospective. Down 6 categories after opening day against Risky Bettiness — six — Jr Jays woke up on Tuesday and apparently decided enough was enough. One day later, they had completely turned it around, and they never looked back. The final score was 9-2-0, with RBI the only dead heat at 36 apiece. Jr Jays posted 11 home runs, 59 strikeouts, 4 quality starts, and a 4.27 ERA against Risky Bettiness's 5.11. Here's the number that still doesn't add up: Risky Bettiness scored 47 runs this week. Forty-seven. Won two categories. Sometimes the league is cruel.

Misio Soup treated Cease and Desist to a week-long tutorial in how this game is supposed to be played, winning 10-2-0 from wire to wire without a moment of suspense. Fifteen home runs. Seventy-seven strikeouts. A .294 team average with a .362 OBP. Cease and Desist won ERA (3.60 to 3.71) and SV+H (7 to 1) and claimed very little else — the OBP gap alone was 0.086, nearly a tenth of a point. This was not competitive. The closing argument was made on day one and the deliberations never got interesting.

Babe Ruthless ran a similarly dominant operation, going 9-3-0 over Ep-Skenes Files from start to finish. Thirteen stolen bases, 36 runs, a strikeout edge of 55 to 41. The one category Ep-Skenes Files could claim in batting was AVG — .215 to .211 — a margin of .004 that was the closest number in the whole matchup. Babe Ruthless answered every challenge on the way to wins in ERA, WHIP, K, QS, R, HR, RBI, SB, and OBP. Ep-Skenes Files salvaged K/BB and SV+H and the knowledge that at least the average was in their corner.

The week's most technically competitive finish came from Professor McGonigle over Eephus, 7-5-0 — a bat versus arm debate that the bat settled on the margins. A .313 average, .389 OBP, and 14 stolen bases from Professor McGonigle against a 2.87 ERA, a 1.04 WHIP, and 3 quality starts from Eephus. OBP was the closest category at a margin of 0.042, which tells you how tight this thing ran. The pitching was better on the Eephus side — no argument there. But 39 runs and 14 steals don't leave enough room for the mound work to catch up, and Professor McGonigle takes the 7-5 and moves on.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Let me be direct with you: a .545 batting average and a .655 OBP in a single week is not a fantasy baseball performance. It is a crime scene. James Wood put up numbers this week that would make Tony Gwynn do a double-take, raking his way through opposing pitching with the kind of authority that reminds you why the Nationals gave him the keys to their rebuild. Eight runs, three home runs, eight RBI, two stolen bases — the man touched every single category. Gordon's Jr Jays didn't deserve this performance. Nobody deserves this performance. The Batter of the Week wasn't even close.

The runners-up deserve their flowers, though. Elly De La Cruz did Elly things for Professor McGonigle — seven runs, four stolen bases, and that .438 OBP that reminds you this kid can do things nobody else on a baseball field can do. Over in the Misio Soup dugout, Dennis got a two-for-one special: Gunnar Henderson went deep four times with seven runs scored, and Jordan Walker quietly posted five home runs and a .360 average. Yes, that Jordan Walker — the one Cardinals fans have been waiting on since 2022. Apparently he decided this was the week. Dennis doesn't need luck when his roster decides to catch fire simultaneously.

Now let's talk about José Soriano, because twenty strikeouts in fifteen innings is the kind of stat line that makes you put the phone down and stare at the wall. A 0.60 ERA. A 0.53 WHIP. A 6.67 K/BB ratio. Two quality starts. Tyler's All Betts Are Off might be onto something with this Angels arm — Soriano has always had the stuff, and this week he looked like a legitimate ace. Logan Gilbert nearly stole the award with a 12.0 K/BB ratio across two quality starts for Misio Soup, and Shota Imanaga's 0.17 WHIP for Juiced Balls is the kind of number that belongs in a video game. But Soriano's week was a masterclass, full stop. Twenty punchouts. Put some respect on the man's name.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Otto Lopez slipped into a Marlins uniform this spring and reminded Ep-Skenes Files why the waiver wire rewards managers who pay attention. A home run, two RBIs, and a .300 average — in a week where most wire adds were scraping together the occasional run, Lopez delivered the rarest commodity in this game. Ep-Skenes Files went to Miami and came back with real fantasy production.

The runners-up made their cases. Eephus added Max Muncy — the veteran who made a career terrorizing NL West pitching during his Dodger years and is now operating in an Athletics uniform — and collected three runs and a .300/.333 line for the week. Professor McGonigle added Colt Keith, who hit .308 out of Detroit, proving the Tigers' youngster earns his spot on a 10-team roster. But the home run is this league's scarcest currency, and Lopez supplied it. Pickup of the week.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

TJ Rumfield is a first baseman playing for Colorado, which means the bar to help your fantasy team is somewhere between "exist" and "breathe." Cease and Desist looked at that formula and said "I'll take it," and Rumfield responded with a .125/.125 line and a complete goose egg across R, HR, RBI, and SB. You play at altitude. Soler has the power to torch an entire fantasy week when he's locked in, but he also has a long history of vanishing completely, and this was one of those disappearing acts. Meanwhile, the real punchline is Konnor Griffin — the PIT prospect that Misio Soup spent $160 in FAAB to acquire this week — who could not scrape together a single hit. That story has more chapters in the Transaction Desk, but the Hall of Shame demands its due: a manager bid eighty percent of his seasonal budget on a prospect who went hitless. Put it in the display case.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Let's start with the trade that has me reaching for the antacids. Babe Ruthless shipped Seiya Suzuki and Nolan McLean to Jr Jays and got Cole Ragans back in return. That's a reliable outfield bat and a legitimate pitching prospect going out the door for one starting pitcher, and the value consolidation needs to deliver. Ragans is a solid arm in Kansas City — mid-rotation, groundball tendencies, decent strikeout rate — but Jr Jays walked away with two productive contributors. Advantage: Jr Jays, unless Ragans pitches like an ace all season.

The other trade is easier to score. Eephus landed Tyler Glasnow from LAD, and Misio Soup received Jarren Duran from BOS. Duran is a legitimate fantasy asset — he runs, he hits, he contributes across the batting categories. But Glasnow, when healthy, is one of the premier strikeout arms in the game, and Eephus is apparently willing to gamble on the "when healthy" part. Misio Soup gets the safer, higher-floor asset. Eephus gets the ceiling play. Slight edge to Eephus if you believe in Glasnow, which I do.

Now about Misio Soup and the $160 Griffin bid. Out of a $200 starting FAAB budget, Misio Soup deployed 80 percent on a PIT prospect who responded with one run, zero hits, and a .250 OBP for the week. The next highest bid on Griffin was $67 from Babe Ruthless. Misio Soup needed to spend $68, not $160. Instead, they detonated their budget on a hitless prospect and now sit at $0 remaining while every other team in this league still has FAAB. The season is three weeks old.

Professor McGonigle closes the ledger with a disciplined $12 pickup of Cam Smith from HOU, beating out a $0 competing bid from a team that no longer has any budget to compete with anyway. With $141 in FAAB remaining, Professor McGonigle is playing chess while half this league plays checkers.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Let's talk about the Cam Smith Hot Potato. Babe Ruthless drops him, Professor McGonigle picks him up for $12. Babe Ruthless currently carries $200 in FAAB — a full wallet, zero budget pressure. When you're holding a full budget and voluntarily releasing a HOU outfielder, that decision requires justification. The justification has not arrived.

The Sabrowski loop is more entertaining. Misio Soup drops Erik Sabrowski to clear a roster spot for the $160 Griffin experiment. Sabrowski is then claimed by Professor McGonigle, who deploys him for one inning, two strikeouts, zero ERA, and a SV+H. Misio Soup spent $160 to acquire a prospect who went hitless and simultaneously gifted a productive reliever to a competitor. That is a complete sequence of events.

Now for the Dead Weight gallery, which this week qualifies as a full exhibit. Juiced Balls is carrying both Salvador Perez at .080/.148 over three weeks and Michael Busch at .050/.091 over the same stretch. Salvy has earned every benefit of the doubt a catcher in this sport has ever been granted, but 25 at-bats of .080 is not a slump — that is a lifestyle. Busch at .050 in 20 at-bats somehow manages to be worse. Two roster slots, two statistical cones, and the waiver wire is not empty.

Cease and Desist is holding Roman Anthony through a .143/.250 stretch — the pedigree demands patience, and frankly I still believe in Anthony. But .143 in 21 at-bats is the kind of number that tests faith. Ep-Skenes Files has the same situation with JJ Wetherholt at .130, another can't-miss prospect who is currently missing. Professor McGonigle rounds out the gallery with Ivan Herrera at .143, though a .379 OBP at least maintains a pulse on the roster card. These managers are rostering hope. Hope is not a scoring category.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Professor McGonigle batted .313 as a team — best in the league — with 14 stolen bases and 76 hits, both also league bests. They also threw 33.1 innings, which is 17 fewer than league average and exactly 0.1 above the threshold that forfeits all pitching categories. They went 7-5. The offense was so dominant that the bare-minimum pitching approach was actually correct, even if it looked like a dare.

Juiced Balls posted a 0.88 WHIP this week. The league average was 1.29. Their 5.09 K/BB ratio led all ten teams — the next closest was 3.63. ERA of 3.15 against a field averaging 4.02. By every rate metric on the mound, they were the best pitching team in the league. They also scored 21 runs and hit 4 home runs — both the worst numbers in the league. Excellent pitching, no offense. They lost.

Risky Bettiness scored 47 runs this week, the league leader. Average was 32.8. Scoring 43 percent above the mean typically produces wins. Not this week. The offense was real — a .378 OBP explains how they manufactured runs without elite power numbers — but the pitching was a 5.11 ERA, 1.46 WHIP, 1.95 K/BB, and one quality start. Jr Jays took every pitching category and most of batting. Those 47 runs won Risky Bettiness exactly one category. Runs are not the same as wins.
