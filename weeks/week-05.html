Week 5 Recap (2026-04-20 — 2026-04-26)

Monday, April 27, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

All Betts Are Off opened against Babe Ruthless down 2-8 after Monday, trailing by six categories with six days left. The lead changed twice before Sunday: All Betts Are Off surged ahead mid-week, Babe Ruthless reclaimed a 7-5 edge by Friday and held it through Saturday, still favored heading into the final day. Then Sunday happened, and five categories flipped on the final day. The week's final ledger — 14 home runs, 45 RBI, a 2.43 ERA, a 1.21 WHIP — turned a week-long grind into a 10-2-0 runaway. Babe Ruthless salvaged stolen bases (8 to 3) and saves-plus-holds. Six categories up Monday. Two wins by Sunday.

Juiced Balls authored a similar final-day escape against Kirby's Dream Team, except this one required five days of patience before the first sign of life. Kirby's Dream Team led from Monday through Saturday on the strength of 9 home runs and 10 stolen bases — categories Juiced Balls simply couldn't match. Sunday changed everything. Juiced Balls took the lead for the first time all week, swept pitching — better ERA, more strikeouts, more saves-plus-holds — and closed out a 6-5-1 win with quality starts deadlocked at three apiece.

The Eephus vs. Ep-Skenes Files matchup produced three lead changes before settling into an 8-4-0 final. Ep-Skenes Files posted a 0.81 WHIP — a genuinely elite number — won K/BB at 3.55, and put up eight saves-plus-holds. Eephus countered by sweeping all six batting categories, including OBP by a .004 margin (.313 to .309), then added five quality starts and a 2.59 ERA to win the pitching split as well. That is a hard matchup to lose when the WHIP looks that good.

Professor McGonigle dug out of a 4-6 hole on Monday to beat Risky Bettiness 8-3-1, and the margin came down to the plate. Risky Bettiness actually won ERA (4.09 to 4.86), WHIP (1.24 to 1.33), and K/BB — legitimate pitching categories — but hit .174 with a .294 OBP, which makes those advantages irrelevant in a twelve-category format. Professor McGonigle answered with better hitting across the board, 8 stolen bases, 11 home runs, and 63 innings of pitching volume. RBI finished a dead tie at 33 apiece.

The other one-category finish: Cease and Desist edged Jr Jays 6-5-1, with stolen bases the deciding razor — both squads finished at exactly four. Jr Jays controlled batting entirely: .311 average, .390 OBP, 16 home runs, 47 runs scored — a dominant hitting week by any measure. Cease and Desist answered by sweeping pitching, including four quality starts to one, six saves-plus-holds to one, and a marginally better ERA (5.40 to 5.44) and WHIP (1.33 to 1.42). Jr Jays led 7-4 after Monday and were firmly in control before Cease and Desist flipped it on Wednesday and never looked back.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Josh Naylor just put up the kind of week that redefines a player's fantasy profile. Four stolen bases from a 240-pound first baseman — four, on a team that usually counts his legs as strictly decorative — paired with a .529 average, and Kirby's Dream Team walks away with Batter of the Week in a race that wasn't close at the top. Naylor has always been a legit run producer in Seattle, but the speed? That's new. File it under pleasant surprises.

The runners-up gave the committee something to chew on. Sal Stewart posted 10 RBIs for Babe Ruthless — double digits in a single week, the kind of production that swings matchups single-handedly — while Max Muncy gave Jr Jays a .583 OBP and three home runs in the same breath. Muncy walking at a historic clip is the closest thing to a cheat code the third-base position offers, and this was a vintage performance. Elly De La Cruz checked in for Professor McGonigle with three homers and eight RBIs and still finished third, which tells you how loaded this week's batter pool actually was.

The pitching crown goes to Nolan McLean, who posted 17 strikeouts and two walks across 11.2 innings for Jr Jays — a K/BB of 8.50 that would make a pitching coach weep. Two walks. In nearly a dozen innings. The young Mets righty is starting to look like more than a streaming option, and this was the week he made the argument loudest. Tyler Glasnow delivered a gem with a 0.25 WHIP for Eephus — when you allow barely a baserunner over eight innings, you've earned your praise — while Emmet Sheehan's 10 strikeouts and 1.42 ERA gave Babe Ruthless another strong arm to lean on. Louis Varland out of the Toronto bullpen converted four saves and holds for Ep-Skenes Files with seven strikeouts in four innings, which is the kind of relief performance that makes a tight matchup feel a lot more comfortable on Monday morning.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

The separation at the top is real, and the gap between these three and the rest of the league is growing by the week.

#1 — All Betts Are Off (40-19-1): A 10-2 beatdown this week, a .675 winning percentage on the season — this roster is playing like it has something to prove. Dominant across categories, no signs of slowing down. The best team in the league, and it's not a conversation anymore.

#2 — Kirby's Dream Team (37-21-2): Dropped a 5-6 decision this week, which is the kind of slip that keeps a lead columnist employed. Still the second-best record in the league by a comfortable margin, and one bad week changes nothing about who this team is. Sleep on them at your own risk.

#3 — Professor McGonigle (31-26-3): The 8-3 win pushes him closer to that second tier, and at .542 he's starting to look like a legitimate postseason threat.

SOLID

Good enough to make noise. Not quite good enough to scare the top three yet.

#4 — Babe Ruthless (33-26-1): That 2-10 loss is a crater. Jordan, last year's champion, just took the worst beating of his season, and the record will feel the bruise. Still the fourth-best winning percentage in the league, but a performance like that raises questions about the pitching staff.

#5 — Juiced Balls (27-30-3): The 6-5 squeaker keeps them alive in the conversation, but at .475 they're right on the fence. Juiced Balls has been dangerously inconsistent — one-category wins don't inspire confidence when the top of the bracket is rolling like this.

MEDIOCRE

Treading water. Staying afloat without gaining any ground.

#6 — Eephus (26-32-2): The 8-4 win is the best single-week result in this tier, and Jesse continues to punch above expectations for a first-year manager. He's not a fluke. Whether he can sustain it is the question nobody's answered yet.

#7 — Jr Jays (25-31-4): Tied with Eephus and Cease and Desist on winning percentage but lost 5-6 this week. Gordon is running out of room to maneuver, and the schedule isn't about to do him any favors.

#8 — Cease and Desist (23-34-3): A 6-5 win isn't exactly a statement, but Philip is taking what the league gives him. The problem is he needs wins in bunches, not trickles, to climb out of this hole before June.

REBUILDING

These teams are well past playoff talk and squarely into damage control.

#9 — Ep-Skenes Files (24-32-4): A 4-8 loss when you're already in the basement is exactly the wrong kind of week. Matt's team has the wins to suggest this isn't a total loss of a season, but the trend line is pointing the wrong direction.

#10 — Risky Bettiness (21-36-3): Three-eight this week, .375 on the year. Nate is in the deepest hole in the league, and there are no easy matchups on the horizon. The season isn't over in late April, but it starts to feel that way when you're nine games under .500.

Movers and Shakers

All Betts Are Off just walked into the building and took the crown. Up two spots to number one overall with a 40-19-1 record. Kirby's Dream Team held the top spot coming in and now sits at number two, which tells you everything you need to know about how competitive the top of this standings is. Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jonathan Aranda put on a power display that made Professor McGonigle look like a genius for nabbing him off the wire. Three home runs, seven RBI, and an OBP of .381 over six days — Tampa Bay's first baseman delivered cleanup-caliber production while most of the league wasn't watching. Aranda has always had the plate discipline, and the power arrived in the right week.

Runners-up worth acknowledging: Dalton Rushing gave Ep-Skenes Files a .300/.462 slash line in five days behind the plate, which counts as a small miracle at the catcher position. And Dansby Swanson delivered six RBI for All Betts Are Off in just four days, the kind of quiet counting stats that flip tight matchups when they're needed most.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Juiced Balls rostered Tony Santillan and received 1.2 innings pitched with a 16.2 ERA for the trouble. Santillan has been cycled through the Reds bullpen for two seasons precisely because he produces these numbers with regularity. Adding him to a fantasy roster in late April is the wire equivalent of hiring a contractor who has already burned down three kitchens.

Brayan Rocchio gave Eephus a .250/.250 slash line — identical average and OBP, meaning he drew zero walks the entire stint — with two runs and two RBI to show for the roster slot. Jacob Wilson did even less for Babe Ruthless, turning in one run and one RBI before being cut loose. The common thread: every one of these managers knew it wasn't working and still waited.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Emmet Sheehan gave Babe Ruthless everything a streamer can give. One start, 6.1 innings, ten strikeouts, a 1.42 ERA, 0.79 WHIP, and a K/BB ratio of 10.0. Sheehan has fought his way back from Tommy John surgery, and when he's on like this — pinpoint command, plus stuff, practically no free passes — he's as good as anyone in the National League. Babe Ruthless identified the spot start, made the add, and walked away with a quality start and a K total that padded every pitching category.

Justin Wrobleski threw six shutout innings with a quality start in a single-day grab by All Betts Are Off, which is exactly how streaming is supposed to work. Kumar Rocker earned a quality start for Eephus on a one-day spot as well. But Sheehan's ten-strikeout performance separates this one from the pack. That kind of K total out of a streaming start is the payoff for knowing who to add and when.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ep-Skenes Files has some explaining to do. Matt McLain sat on the bench for three days he had games and put up four runs, two home runs, five RBI and a stolen base from the pine. Noah Schultz sat the entire week and delivered six innings, eight strikeouts, and a quality start without ever getting a look. Two contributors, both producing, both watching from the bench. Ep-Skenes Files is rostering talent and then managing around it.

Eephus matched that energy on the batting side. Angel Martínez sat two days he had stats and launched three home runs while driving in five. Jo Adell rode the bench for five days and chipped in a homer and four RBI anyway. That's two live power bats buried behind presumably less productive active roster options. Babe Ruthless left Cole Ragans on the bench for his one start — six innings, eleven strikeouts, a quality start from one of the better left-handers in the American League. Eleven strikeouts sitting unused.

Kirby's Dream Team benched Konnor Griffin two days he had games — one homer, four RBI, two steals went uncounted. Risky Bettiness kept Liam Hicks on the bench all week while he put up three runs, a home run and five RBI. Seven bench blunders across the league in a single week. The autopilot is set too high.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

One bid. One dollar. That's the complete FAAB activity for the week in Dad's Baseball League 9.0. Professor McGonigle, carrying $140 of the original $200 budget after a busy early season, spent $1 to swap Jeffrey Springs for Landen Roupp out of San Francisco. Roupp responded by earning a quality start with a 3.52 ERA and a 0.52 WHIP across seven-plus innings — a solid return on a minimum bid from a manager with plenty of ammunition left to spend.

The frozen war chests around the rest of the league tell their own story. Six teams are sitting at the full $200, completely untouched through the first month of the season. Nobody has spotted anything worth bidding on, or nobody has been looking. Kirby's Dream Team is at the other extreme — $0 remaining before April is even finished. Every waiver add from here forward is a $0 blind bid, which means losing any contested pickup to whoever throws real money behind it. When the stretch run hits in August and rosters need reinforcement, that empty budget will be the most expensive mistake made in April.

The Insider Report

by "Deep Source" DiNapoli

Sources are telling me that Kirby's Dream Team is dangling George Kirby. You read that right — the ace who the team is literally named after is reportedly on the trade block, and this raises an uncomfortable pattern: sources close to the front office suggest that whatever star player lends his name to this franchise is, eventually, available. Consider it the unofficial DiNapoli Theorem of team-naming: if you're on the jersey, you're probably in the rumour mill. Kirby's Dream Team sits at 37-21-2, third in the league — so this isn't a teardown. They're shopping from a position of strength, which means the asking price is going to hurt.

The question is who bites. A right-handed starting pitcher with Kirby's strikeout profile is exactly what the teams behind them in the standings need to close the gap, and at 3rd place with their record, they can afford to be patient. My money says this deal doesn't happen cheap, and it probably doesn't happen at all unless someone panics. Keep an eye on Juiced Balls and Eephus — both hovering around .450-.475 and in desperate need of a rotation anchor to push themselves into the conversation.

Meanwhile, my sources inside the Professor McGonigle front office describe the situation as... spirited. The debate over the middle infield is apparently ongoing: one faction says the roster is bloated at the position, another insists names like Bo Bichette, Jose Altuve, and Willy Adames are out there and need to be targeted. These are not small names — and if the Bichette/Altuve/Adames camp wins the argument, someone currently on that roster is headed out the door to make room. The $1 FAAB bid on Landen Roupp this week suggests the front office can agree on low-cost pitching fliers, at least. Agreement on everything else? That's another story.

Here's where it gets interesting: Professor McGonigle at 31-26-3 and Kirby's Dream Team at 37-21-2 are both contenders. One has a pitcher to move, the other has a surplus of middle infielders and needs to consolidate. There is a trade to be made here. Whether the front offices can get out of their own way long enough to make it happen is the question I'll be watching. Stay tuned — this one has the feel of a deal that gets done right before the deadline, or not at all.

DiNapoli out. Don't @ me unless you've got receipts.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Jr Jays led the league in runs (47), home runs (16), batting average (.311), and on-base percentage (.390) this week, taking five of six batting categories against Cease and Desist. They still lost, 6-5-1. One quality start and one save-plus-hold; Cease and Desist swept all six pitching categories. The best offensive week in the league, and it was not enough.

Ep-Skenes Files ran the opposite experiment. Best WHIP in the league at 0.81, best K/BB at 3.55, eight saves and holds. Their pitching staff was excellent. Their lineup hit .218 with three home runs and 26 runs scored, both league-worst marks. Eephus swept all six batting categories and claimed two pitching categories, winning 8-4. A 0.81 WHIP does not hit home runs.

All Betts Are Off won 10 of 12 categories against Babe Ruthless: league-best 2.43 ERA, 45 RBI, 14 home runs, .291 average. They lost stolen bases and saves-plus-holds. That kind of balanced production across both halves of the roster is why the standings read 10-2.

Babe Ruthless logged 70.1 innings this week — most in the league by a significant margin, nearly 16 above average. The return: 3.84 ERA, 1.46 WHIP, and the worst K/BB in the league at 2.10. Three quality starts from 70 innings pitched is a lot of work for a middling pitching week. They lost every pitching category to the team they faced. In H2H categories, the standings pay by the category won, not by the inning thrown.
