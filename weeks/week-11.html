Week 11 Recap (2026-06-01 — 2026-06-07)

Monday, June 8, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Nobody saw this coming when Day 1 closed with Ep-Skenes Files up eleven categories to zero against Kirby's Dream Team. By midweek, that lead was gone. Kirby's Dream Team took the batting column — 38 runs, a .276 average, 6 steals on the week — and moved ahead 7-5, holding that edge into the final day. Then the pitching categories swung. The week finished at a 0.93 WHIP, 2.60 ERA, and a 5.62 K/BB ratio in the Ep-Skenes Files rotation, and when the last day's numbers settled, the match had flipped to 7-5-0. Two lead changes, a two-category hole erased on the final day — Kirby's Dream Team had this one in hand and let it go.

The Bryce Is Right handled Jr Jays from the first pitch of the week. Cumulative category totals after Day 1: Bryce 12, Jr Jays zero. The 9-3-0 final actually reflects some Jr Jays resilience — they claimed WHIP, quality starts, and saves-plus-holds — but the offensive column was an absolute clinic. Forty-six runs, 12 home runs, 39 RBI, 11 steals, a .280 average. The Bryce Is Right didn't record a single save or hold all week and still won nine categories. Wire-to-wire. Never in doubt.

The league's most unsatisfying result: Professor McGonigle and Babe Ruthless split 6-6-0, with batting average the difference — Babe Ruthless at .239 to Professor McGonigle's .227. Babe Ruthless posted the better offensive week — 37 runs, 9 home runs, 25 RBI — and Professor McGonigle answered with elite pitching: 59 strikeouts, a 3.00 ERA, a 1.19 WHIP. Six categories apiece, no winner, everyone walks away frustrated. These two teams deserved a decision. They didn't get one.

Cease and Desist survived a 6.12 ERA and just one quality start to beat Risky Bettiness 7-5-0. How? Fifty-seven strikeouts, 30 RBI, and the AVG battle by five points — .261 to .256. Risky Bettiness owned the pitching quality (3.93 ERA, 1.23 WHIP, 2 quality starts) but couldn't close the batting gap. Cease and Desist wins ugly. Ugly still counts.

Juiced Balls took down Eephus 7-3-2, with two categories finishing dead even — RBI (39-39) and steals (6-6). Eephus edged out runs (37-33) and home runs (11-10), and won the K/BB battle at 4.20 to 3.53. That's three wins and two ties against Juiced Balls' seven. But Juiced Balls swept most of the pitching column and posted a .291 average, and in a 12-category league, breadth beats depth. Eephus just ran out of categories to convert.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Pete Crow-Armstrong just told everyone who thought he was a glove-first center fielder to take a seat. Four home runs, six RBI, two steals, a .429 average, .478 OBP — the 24-year-old Cub went line to line and Ep-Skenes Files walks away with the Batter of the Week in a landslide. The first-round pick out of the 2020 draft — taken by the Mets and flipped to Chicago in the Javier Báez deal — was always supposed to be this kind of player; this week the bat finally made the argument the tools always promised.

Chase Burns earns the Pitcher of the Week for The Bryce Is Right: six innings, nine strikeouts, a 0.83 WHIP, and a K/BB ratio of 9.0. One walk in six innings. Burns was Cincinnati's 2024 first-round pick, a high-ceiling arm whose command was the last piece to show up — and a 9-to-1 strikeout-to-walk ratio in a quality start is the "he figured it out" performance the league has been waiting on.

The runners-up earned their ink. Yordan Alvarez posted .476/.577 with nine RBI for Kirby's Dream Team — that OBP belongs on a trophy, and in most weeks it wins this column outright. Brice Turang hit .407 with two homers, eight RBI, and two steals for Risky Bettiness, touching all six batting categories. Jackson Chourio scored eight runs for Juiced Balls. On the mound, Jacob deGrom threw eleven innings, struck out fourteen, and posted a 0.91 WHIP for Juiced Balls — a locked-in deGrom is still one of the most dangerous arms in this format. Joe Ryan gave Risky Bettiness twelve innings, fourteen strikeouts, and a 3.75 ERA across his starts. And Jhoan Duran struck out seven batters in three innings while picking up three saves-plus-holds for Professor McGonigle — that's what closing the door looks like.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

The top three have separated themselves from the rest of this league, and at this point the only question is order. The Bryce Is Right moves to the top spot this week — a 9-3-0 demolition of Jr Jays while everyone else in the league stumbled will do that. Tyler's outfit now sits at 79-48-5, and the 9-3-0 performance wasn't a fluke; this team keeps finding ways to cover categories when it matters.

Professor McGonigle owns the best winning percentage in the league at .636 and 81 wins, but a 6-6-0 tie drags him down a peg this week. Tom has been the model of consistency all season — you don't get to 81 wins by accident — but you don't hold the top spot by drawing ties against .549 competition either.

Kirby's Dream Team drops to three after a 5-7-0 loss to Ep-Skenes Files. The record still looks like a contender at 76-51-5, and a .595 winning percentage keeps Dennis firmly in this conversation, but a loss to a fourth-place team when you need to be separating yourself from the pack is not the week the doctor ordered.

SOLID

Two teams sitting just outside the top tier with real shots at a playoff run. Ep-Skenes Files put together the win of the week from this group, beating a top-three team 7-5-0. Matt's 71-56-5 record and .557 winning percentage aren't flashy, but the team just proved it can go punch-for-punch with the contenders when it needs to.

Babe Ruthless at 70-57-5 deserves credit for holding Professor McGonigle to a 6-6-0 draw — you don't walk away from a matchup with the winningest team in the league having split categories evenly and feel bad about it. Jordan is sitting on a .549 clip and built this tier to be a danger zone heading into the second half.

MEDIOCRE

Three teams that are too good to quit on but too inconsistent to trust. Juiced Balls gets the top spot here after a 7-3-2 win over Eephus — first time in a while these guys have been worth talking about in positive terms, and a .451 record needs a lot more of those weeks to matter come playoff time. Jr Jays ranks second in this group on record alone at 55-69-8, but getting run over 9-3-0 by The Bryce Is Right is an ugly way to spend a week. Cease and Desist rounds out the tier after a 7-5-0 win; the .424 winning percentage is the worst in the group by a wide margin, but wins are wins and Philip is at least moving in the right direction.

REBUILDING

Someone has to be last and this week it's the same two names. Risky Bettiness at 43-82-7 is the worst record in the league — Nate dropped a 5-7-0 decision to Cease and Desist, which is the kind of loss that defines a rebuilding season. Eephus holds the nine spot, though a 3-7-2 loss is at least slightly less catastrophic than a straight 3-9-0; Jesse's team is at 46-80-6 and the only thing separating this squad from last place is that Risky Bettiness exists.

Movers and Shakers

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The Bryce Is Right have quietly climbed to second place with a 79-48-5 record, and there's nothing quiet about what that number says. Eleven weeks into the season and they're sitting one spot off the top with a winning percentage that belongs in a serious championship conversation. Meanwhile, Kirby's Dream Team slips to third — still a formidable 76-51-5 — but sliding in a pennant race has a way of feeling worse than the numbers suggest, and The Hammer is watching closely.

Down below the fold, Babe Ruthless drops to fifth at 70-57-5, a concerning drift for the defending champion. Jordan won this league as a rookie manager last season — the pedigree is there — but fifth place is not a place you want to be when the calendar starts filling up. Ep-Skenes Files surges to fourth and is nipping at the heels of the top tier, while in the bottom half, Juiced Balls and Jr Jays are essentially trading places at sixth and seventh, both hovering right around 55 wins, both below .500, both running out of runway to make this season mean something.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ep-Skenes Files wins the week's pickup honors with JJ Bleday, and the bar was low enough that this still counts as a legitimate crown. Bleday delivered a homer, a stolen base, and two RBI — four categories touched, which is the kind of cross-category utility you're hunting for when you're trolling the wire mid-week. The .154 average and .185 OBP are not going to win any batting categories, but four-category contributions from a free agent have value regardless of how they look in the box score.

The former fourth overall pick came into the league as a polished college bat projected for contact and power. The contact remains a work in progress — this batting average is evidence of that — but the power showed up when it counted. In a quiet week on the waiver wire, Ep-Skenes Files found what was there to find.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jack Leiter took the mound for Kirby's Dream Team and delivered 4 2/3 innings of suffering: 2 strikeouts, a 9.64 ERA, a 1.93 WHIP, and a 0.67 K/BB ratio — meaning he walked more batters than he struck out. Leiter arrived in the majors carrying the weight of a famous last name; his father Al threw big games for the Marlins and Mets for a decade. The pedigree offered no protective value this week.

Eduardo Rodriguez handed Jr Jays 6 1/3 innings with a 5.68 ERA and didn't earn a quality start. The walk rate was fine — five strikeouts against one walk — but the hits piled up and the ERA says everything. Rodriguez has flashed legitimate frontline stuff at points in his career, which makes this line feel like a wasted roster move. And then there's JJ Bleday — the same JJ Bleday who claimed this week's Best Pickup of the Week — logging a .154 average and .185 OBP for Ep-Skenes Files. A homer saves him from full Hall of Shame induction, but the batting line earns him an honorable mention. When the same player appears on both lists in the same week, the front office has had a complicated seven days.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Roki Sasaki gave Ep-Skenes Files seven innings, 10 strikeouts, a 0.57 WHIP, and nothing on the ERA line. He earned a quality start, commanded the zone with a 5.00 K/BB ratio, and made it look effortless. The Dodgers' signing out of Japan has legitimate front-of-the-rotation stuff on his best days, and this was one of his best days.

Sandy Alcantara pitched seven innings for Babe Ruthless — a 1.29 ERA, 0.86 WHIP, seven strikeouts against one walk, a quality start. Alcantara is the kind of pitcher who eats innings without giving ground, and this was a textbook outing. Michael Soroka contributed 13 innings across multiple appearances for Jr Jays — 12 strikeouts, a 3.46 ERA, 0.92 WHIP, and a quality start — delivering real value through volume. Soroka pieced himself back together after a string of injuries that would have ended most careers, and he keeps showing up.

Sasaki wins the week. Ten strikeouts, seven innings, nothing on the board. The others pitched well. Sasaki pitched a gem.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The single worst bench decision this week came from Risky Bettiness, who sat Liam Hicks for the entire week — every day, all seven — while Hicks produced four runs, a homer, and two RBI. Not a day off because of a favorable matchup. Not a spot-start situation. Every. Single. Day. The definition of a bench blunder is leaving production on the table; this is leaving the whole meal on the table and locking the door on the way out.

Kirby's Dream Team benched Oneil Cruz for two of his active days and watched him put up three runs, a home run, three RBI, and a stolen base from the sidelines. Cruz is one of the most explosive shortstops in the game when healthy — speed, power, and the occasional jaw-dropping defensive play. Benching him twice in a week is the kind of decision that shows up in final standings at the worst possible time. The Bryce Is Right gave Willson Contreras three days off while the veteran catcher produced five hits, two runs, a home run, and three RBI. That is significant production drain at an already-thin position. Cease and Desist benched Kazuma Okamoto for two days, missing a homer and a pair of RBI. Small numbers in isolation, but in a matchup decided by a category or two, those are the numbers that haunt a front office.

Ep-Skenes Files managed to bench both Pete Crow-Armstrong and Jose Altuve on days when each went deep. Crow-Armstrong homered and drove in two; Altuve scored twice and hit his own homer. Two missed home runs in one week from the same roster is a front office problem, not bad luck. Altuve has been producing at this level for over a decade and is still finding ways to hurt pitchers — he's earned a starting spot on days he's active. So has Crow-Armstrong, one of the Cubs' most electrifying young pieces. Ep-Skenes Files had a busy week on the waiver wire and a quieter week in their own lineup than they should have.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Ep-Skenes Files posted the best ERA (2.60), WHIP (0.93), K/BB (5.62), strikeout total (73), and SV+H (7) in the league this week — five of six pitching categories, measured against a field averaging 4.16 ERA, 1.21 WHIP, 3.42 K/BB, and 53.7 strikeouts. They also threw 65 2/3 innings, the most of any team. None of it produced a blowout: the matchup finished 7-5-0 because Kirby's Dream Team won five of six batting categories — runs, RBI, stolen bases, AVG, and OBP.

That batting performance deserves its own accounting. Kirby recorded 62 strikeouts from their pitching staff — the sole second-highest total in the league — and 12 home runs, tied for second in the league. Both of those categories went to Ep-Skenes. Their WHIP was 1.39, the worst mark this week, and their ERA sat at 4.92. Five batting categories won, six pitching categories lost. The final score reflects the arithmetic.

The Bryce Is Right went 9-3-0 with zero saves-plus-holds — the only team in the league to record a goose egg there, against a league average of 3.60. They led the league in runs (46) and stolen bases (11), tied for the league lead in RBI at 39, and hit .280 with a .354 OBP. The three categories that went to Jr Jays were WHIP, quality starts, and saves-plus-holds. When a lineup is operating at that level, conceding one relief category becomes a rounding error on the final score.
