Week 9 Recap (2026-05-18 — 2026-05-24)

Monday, May 25, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Kirby's Dream Team was down six categories going into the weekend, with The Bryce Is Right holding a 9-3 lead through Thursday and the week all but written off. Then the final two days happened. Kirby ground it down, and Sunday delivered the flip — a final score of 6-4-2, the most improbable comeback of the young season. The Bryce Is Right won batting average by three thousandths of a point (.235 to .232) but gave back OBP by 43 points, and Kirby's edge in runs, homers, and steals, plus the strikeout and ERA columns, carried the rest. Down six. Then not.

Ep-Skenes Files delivered the week's most comprehensive beatdown: a 12-0 perfect score against Risky Bettiness, wire-to-wire, never closer than 9-2 at any point in the week. The supplemental embarrassment: Risky Bettiness finished at 26.2 innings, below the 30-inning minimum, forfeiting all six pitching categories automatically. Whether the forfeit changed the outcome is beside the point — Risky was losing all six on the numbers anyway, a 4.39 ERA and 1.24 WHIP going up against Ep-Skenes' 2.74 ERA and 60 strikeouts across 65 innings. The forfeit was gratuitous. Lose the pitching side on merit, fine. Lose it on a technicality because nobody streamed a spot starter to clear the floor — that's a different kind of failure.

Professor McGonigle handled Juiced Balls 9-2-1 the easy way: let the opponent's 5.94 ERA and 1.53 WHIP surrender the entire pitching side of the card. The Professor swept all six pitching categories, cleared 9 home runs and 31 RBI, and piled up 11 saves and holds. Juiced Balls won AVG and OBP — both by thin margins — and that was the extent of the resistance. Cease and Desist, meanwhile, posted 72 strikeouts in a 9-3-0 win over Eephus. Seventy-two. Eephus answered with 9 home runs and 10 saves and holds, but Cease and Desist doubled the strikeout total, swept ERA, WHIP, K/BB, and QS alongside it, and there's no batting-column comeback from a deficit that wide.

Babe Ruthless beat Jr Jays 8-3-1 in a wire-to-wire week that was never particularly close — at one point the cumulative score read 12-0 before Jr Jays salvaged Ks (51 to 50), saves and holds, and Runs to close it out. The Jr Jays rotation posted a 7.26 ERA and 1.63 WHIP, six pitching categories handed over before anyone touched a bat. Babe Ruthless wasn't dominant at the plate — their own 4.55 ERA isn't a staff worth bragging about — but a 7.26 ERA opponent has a way of making everything else look fine.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ketel Marte hit .536 this week. Three home runs. Twelve RBI. Eleven runs scored. The Diamondbacks second baseman — the same switch-hitter who turned himself into an NLCS legend in 2023, a contact machine with real pop in both directions — put together one of the premier individual batting performances of the season for The Bryce Is Right. The .562 OBP tells you he wasn't just swinging at fastballs; he was locked in and patient at the same time. Runners-up Nick Kurtz (.423 AVG, .545 OBP for Cease and Desist), William Contreras (.458 AVG, .500 OBP for the same squad), and Teoscar Hernández (.350 AVG, two home runs, eight RBI for Risky Bettiness) all delivered legitimate weeks. They just happen to live in Marte's shadow this time around.

Jacob Misiorowski threw thirteen innings, struck out twenty batters, and posted a 0.69 ERA, a 0.54 WHIP, and a 10.0 K/BB ratio over two quality starts. Professor McGonigle deployed the Brewers right-hander at his absolute peak — Misiorowski has been one of the most buzzed-about arms in the Milwaukee system, a big fastball paired with the kind of control that makes those strikeout numbers mean something, and this week he was everything the hype promised. Twenty punchouts in two starts with barely anyone reaching base. For the runners-up: Zebby Matthews logged twelve innings with twelve strikeouts and a 12.0 K/BB for Ep-Skenes Files, Kyle Harrison put up a 0.43 WHIP in seven innings and earned a quality start for The Bryce Is Right, and Cade Smith contributed four saves-plus-holds out of the Cleveland bullpen for Professor McGonigle. Fine work all around — but Misiorowski pitched his way onto a different list this week.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

Three teams have separated from the pack, and the daylight between them and the rest of the field keeps growing.

1. Professor McGonigle — Tom goes 9-2 this week and sits alone at 68-34-6, the best record in the league by a significant margin. He runs this operation like he's managing the '98 Yankees — methodical, relentless, and so far ahead that the rest of the league is effectively playing for second.

2. Ep-Skenes Files — Twelve wins, zero losses. Matt went a perfect 12-0 this week and vaults to the two-spot on the strength of the most dominant single-week performance the league has seen all year. The season record still trails Kirby's, but a 12-0 week earns the ranking.

3. Kirby's Dream Team — Dennis goes 6-4 and holds the contender label, sitting at a solid .597 for the year. Watching Ep-Skenes go 12-0 in the same week is either motivating or humbling — probably both.

SOLID

Legitimate playoff teams who can't quite crack the top tier, and the gap is getting harder to ignore.

4. Babe Ruthless — Jordan goes 8-3 and stays in contention at 58-46-4. The 2025 champion isn't done, but closing the gap on the three teams above him is going to require a sharper gear than he's shown recently.

5. The Bryce Is Right — Tyler owns a .611 winning percentage, second-best in the entire league, and still sits fifth because the teams above him have been playing at another level. A 4-6 loss this week didn't help the case.

MEDIOCRE

Still alive in the standings, but the margin for error is gone.

6. Cease and Desist — Philip goes 9-3 in his best week of the season and earns a real acknowledgment for it. The 41-62-5 ledger means one strong week can't rescue the year, but at least the front office showed it can turn it on when it matters.

7. Jr Jays — Gordon drops to 3-8 and the year stands at 44-56-8. Eight ties. Gordon has mastered the art of splitting categories without winning them, and the scoreboard has zero pity for almost.

8. Juiced Balls — A 2-9 week for Fantasy Guru, sitting at 43-59-6 overall. The Guru came in with a reputation, and right now the numbers are doing nothing to protect it.

REBUILDING

The playoff math stopped working weeks ago. It's about pride now.

9. Eephus — Jesse's first season is turning into a hard education. A 3-9 week and 39-65-4 on the year — the learning curve is steep, and the schedule has offered no sympathy.

10. Risky Bettiness — Zero wins, twelve losses. A perfect 0-12. At 35-66-7 overall, Nate is staring at one of the rougher records in nine years of this league, and the calendar still has miles to go.

Movers and Shakers

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The biggest mover this week is the biggest loser, and I mean that literally. Risky Bettiness drops two spots to dead last at 35-66-7, and that record tells you everything you need to know about how this season has gone. Tenth place. Floor seats. The bottom of the barrel. Meanwhile, Cease and Desist climbs two rungs to eighth, which sounds like cause for celebration until you notice the 41-62-5 cumulative record hanging around their neck like an anchor. Progress is progress, but eighth place in a ten-team league with a record like that is still a structure fire — just a slightly smaller one than last week.

At the top of the contender line, Ep-Skenes Files keeps clawing upward, jumping a spot to fourth with a 59-44-5 mark. They're knocking on the door of the top three and deserve credit for the sustained push. Babe Ruthless slides one notch to fifth at 58-46-4, which isn't a collapse — the gap between them is razor thin — but in this league you don't want to be trending the wrong direction heading into the summer grind. Four spots separate second from sixth. Somebody has to separate from the pack. The question is who blinks first.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Casey Schmitt went .500/.600 with a home run, three RBI, and three runs scored, and Kirby's Dream Team should be feeling good about the waiver wire right now. The Giants' utility infielder has always carried the reputation of a player who can go on a tear without warning — the kind of versatile piece who slides from first to second to third while quietly torching opposing pitching. When Schmitt gets hot, he goes cold fast too, so catching him at the right moment is half the battle. This week, the timing was perfect.

The runners-up were respectable. Professor McGonigle went to Washington and found Daylen Lile, the young Nationals outfielder who crossed the plate three times, stole a base, and flashed enough upside to make the move worthwhile. Jr Jays plugged in Spencer Steer at .286 with four runs — solid, workmanlike production from the Reds corner bat. Both managers made sensible calls. Neither one was Schmitt.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The Kenley Jansen reclamation project has claimed another victim. Eephus spotted the 38-year-old Detroit reliever on the wire and decided this was the week he'd rediscover his closer form — and Jansen responded with one inning of work, three earned runs, zero strikeouts, and a 27.0 ERA. The man had a decade of elite, dominant seasons as one of the best closers in the National League. That legacy deserves respect. The 2026 version belongs nowhere near a fantasy roster in late May.

Jr Jays took a shot at Gabriel Moreno, the Arizona catcher who has frustrated fantasy managers for three seasons running with a bat that promises more than it delivers. One run, a .143 average, and a stolen base. Then there's Cease and Desist, who added Jacob Latz and received nothing in exchange — zero innings pitched, zero statistical contribution of any kind. Latz didn't throw a pitch. At least Jansen showed up to the mound. That clears the bar for effort, if nothing else.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Zebby Matthews delivered two starts for Ep-Skenes Files this week that belong in the streaming hall of fame. Twelve innings pitched, twelve strikeouts, a 3.75 ERA, a 0.92 WHIP, and a 12.0 K/BB ratio — meaning Minnesota's young right-hander worked clean, missed bats consistently, and gave his manager almost no reason to sweat across two quality starts. Matthews has been building toward this kind of performance all season, and the Twins are giving him innings to grow into. This week he earned every one of them.

Logan Henderson was a legitimate stream for Kirby's Dream Team, throwing five innings of shutout ball with seven strikeouts and a 1.00 WHIP. He didn't go deep enough to earn a quality start, but the ratios were there. Matthews was operating at a different level, though — a 12-strikeout, two-quality-start week from a waiver wire arm is exactly the kind of find that tilts a categories matchup, and Ep-Skenes Files recognized it in time to act.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Risky Bettiness managed to fail on multiple fronts simultaneously this week, which takes a certain kind of commitment. Twenty-six and two-thirds innings pitched. The minimum is thirty. All six pitching categories — K, ERA, WHIP, K/BB, QS, SV+H — forfeited outright. Worth noting: the pitching matchup was already lost anyway, so the forfeit was a formality built on top of a collapse. Just a complete organizational meltdown from the mound.

If that were the whole story, it would be enough. But Risky Bettiness also benched Liam Hicks for the entire week while Hicks quietly posted two home runs, three runs, and four RBI. Then Cade Cavalli sat on the bench through seven innings, nine strikeouts, and a quality start — unused. A pitcher who earned a quality start, gathering dust. The IP minimum failure is a policy violation. The Hicks and Cavalli decisions are a strategy problem. Together, they read like a franchise that lost the week before it started.

The Bryce Is Right committed the segment's most expensive single oversight: Willson Contreras sat on the bench all week while producing two home runs, eight RBI, four runs, and eight hits. Contreras has a long enough track record as a legitimate offensive catcher that this kind of benching requires an explanation. The team also left a Gavin Williams start on the table — eight innings, eleven strikeouts, and a quality start from a pitcher who clearly had his best stuff. Those are categories that went unearned.

Eephus benched Brandon Nimmo while he produced two home runs, four runs, and five hits, and Juiced Balls let Drew Rasmussen's six-strikeout, seven-inning quality start sit untouched on the bench. Checking the lineup is the bare minimum of this job, and this week, several managers declined to clear it.

The Insider Report

by "Deep Source" DiNapoli

Sources have been buzzing all week, and the name I keep hearing is Trey Yesavage. Multiple front offices have reportedly opened side channels trying to establish a price, and the answer coming back is the same every time: the ask is steep and the current holder isn't blinking. I'm told early offers have been rejected without much deliberation — this is a front office that knows what it has. The longer they hold the line, the more desperate a contender gets, and desperation has a way of driving prices to places nobody expected.

Over at Kirby's Dream Team, the infield situation has apparently reached a crisis level that my sources describe as "creative problem-solving" — which is a generous way to put it. I'm told the front office has grown so desperate for positional flexibility that they've essentially begun petitioning the Seattle Mariners and Pittsburgh Pirates to play Randy Arozarena at second base and Oneil Cruz at third. The Mariners and Pirates have, reportedly, not responded. At 62-41-5 and sitting third in the league, the Dream Team has the record of a contender and the infield of a fever dream. A trade is the obvious solution — the question is whether the front office admits it before the standings force their hand.

Meanwhile, Dylan Cease's injury has apparently shaken loose some real conversations inside Cease and Desist. I'm hearing that despite coming off a win, the front office is facing the uncomfortable math of holding an injured ace on a 41-62-5 roster. Cease is likely the reason this team exists in its current form — he's in the name, for goodness sake — but sentiment doesn't win categories. Selling now, while the name still commands value on the open market, makes more sense than waiting to see if he can return healthy. A contender looking for rotation depth would be smart to make a call.

The natural trade partners for any Cease deal are clustered at the top: Professor McGonigle, The Bryce Is Right, and Ep-Skenes Files all have the rotation needs and positional surplus to make something work. As for Yesavage, I'm told at least one team in the top five of the standings is involved in those conversations, and the number being discussed is not a modest one. I can't confirm names yet — my source was very clear about that — but something is moving. Stay close to your phones.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Risky Bettiness threw 26 2/3 innings this week, 3 1/3 short of the 30-inning floor, and forfeited all six pitching categories. Across those frames they struck out 41 batters, a pace of roughly 13.8 K/9 with a 4.10 K/BB that sat well above the league average of 3.32. The rates were there; the volume wasn't. Against Ep-Skenes Files, the forfeit was almost beside the point, since the batting categories were already going 6-for-6 in Ep-Skenes' favor, but the pitching numbers disappear from the ledger either way.

The Bryce Is Right posted the league's best WHIP (0.87) and K/BB (6.00) this week and still dropped the matchup 4-6-2 to Kirby's Dream Team. The ERA split tells the story: Kirby ran a 2.03 against The Bryce Is Right's 3.02, despite the WHIP disadvantage. Lower traffic, more damage per frame. WHIP measures baserunners per inning; ERA measures runs per nine. When those diverge, sequencing is usually the culprit: hits clustering in the same innings, runners stranding in others. Results won the category.

Cease and Desist led the league in H/AB (63), RBI (41), and pitching strikeouts (72) in the same week. The K total is the standout: league average sat at 49.2, with the next-closest team at 60. They threw 58 innings, so the counting stat had a volume component — but the rate justified it. They won 9 of 12 categories against Eephus, who finished the week with 10 saves-plus-holds, a 2.56 ERA, and nine home runs. On most weeks that's a competitive line. This week, Cease and Desist just ran away with it.
