Week 4 Recap (2026-04-13 — 2026-04-19)

Monday, April 20, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The week's best drama was not on the score sheet — it was in the category swings. Risky Bettiness had Juiced Balls right where they wanted them through the first three days, sitting comfortably ahead 7-5 in categories. Then Thursday flipped everything: Juiced Balls swung four categories in a single day and seized an 8-4 lead, the kind of gut-punch reversal that breaks most teams. Risky Bettiness didn't blink — they came back Friday, retook the lead, and held on for a 7-5-0 final decided by WHIP, where both teams posted exactly 1.23 and someone in Yahoo's backend had to break the tie by a margin that doesn't exist at three decimal places.

The power-and-ERA combination won it. Risky Bettiness crushed 19 home runs and posted a 2.48 ERA — that ERA alone is worth multiple categories before a single at-bat is counted. Juiced Balls countered with 55 strikeouts and 10 stolen bases, respectable enough on paper, but a 4.31 ERA hands categories back with both hands. You can pile up K's all week; give back that many runs and the win column doesn't cooperate.

All Betts Are Off finished off Professor McGonigle 8-4-0, but this one had a subplot: Professor McGonigle actually led 6-5 on April 15th and briefly looked like they had a pulse. It lasted one day. All Betts Are Off retook control the next morning and built to a 9-3 lead by Saturday before settling at 8-4 final. The offense was operating at a different level — .301 average, .394 OBP, 49 runs, 15 home runs. Professor McGonigle wrung 87 strikeouts out of their rotation and still posted a 6.19 ERA, which is a very specific kind of suffering.

Babe Ruthless put away Eephus 9-3-0, and by April 15th this matchup had reached 11 categories to zero — wire-to-wire dominance taken to its logical extreme. Eephus actually outperformed them on ERA (3.45 to Babe Ruthless's 6.01), WHIP (1.04 to 1.45), and K/BB, which tells you exactly how far ahead the bats were carrying this thing. Fifteen home runs, 44 RBI, 43 runs. The 6.01 ERA is a genuine concern for the weeks ahead, but this week the lineup just steamrolled everything in front of it.

Ep-Skenes Files handled Cease and Desist 8-3-0 and never gave them a day of hope. The pitching line did the work: 0.99 WHIP — sub-1.00, which doesn't happen often — paired with a 2.76 ERA and eight saves-plus-holds. Cease and Desist won quality starts six to two and led on strikeouts 68 to 48, but losing ERA, WHIP, and K/BB to those numbers puts you in a hole from opening day. No lead changes, no drama, just a clean operation.

Misio Soup dispatched Jr Jays 8-4-0 on the strength of a rotation that posted a 2.10 ERA and 1.12 WHIP, locking up all four pitching ratio categories before the week had any momentum. The closest thing to suspense was batting average: Misio Soup edged Jr Jays by .002, the kind of margin you need a calculator to care about. Jr Jays actually out-struck them on the mound (66 K's to 61) and won on RBI, but that ERA gap runs through the whole board. Not a great week for Jr Jays to figure out.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

José Ramírez did what José Ramírez does — he touched all five batting categories like he was checking off a grocery list and left the rest of the league wondering why they don't own him. Nine runs, four home runs, five steals, a .364 average, and a .533 OBP. All Betts Are Off just watched JRam put up a line that most teams don't produce collectively, and the rest of us had to sit back and respect it. This is a guy who has been a top-five fantasy asset for half a decade for a reason — he doesn't have a weak category, and weeks like this are why his auction price never comes down.

The runner-up conversation is genuinely brutal. Mike Trout went out there and hit five home runs with nine RBI and nine runs scored for Risky Bettiness — a line that wins Batter of the Week in 49 out of 50 weeks — and it wasn't enough. Nico Hoerner drove in eleven runs for Professor McGonigle, which is the kind of RBI week that should be getting banner headlines. José Caballero chipped in four steals and a .360 average for Juiced Balls doing the speed-and-average thing that makes him so dangerous. Three guys who would've owned this award in a normal week, and Ramírez made them all afterthoughts.

Mason Miller gets the pitching nod and it's not hard to see why — eight strikeouts in four innings with a 0.50 WHIP, an 8:1 K/BB ratio, and four saves or holds for Juiced Balls is the kind of dominant closer work that reminds everyone his stuff is genuinely unfair. Paul Skenes pitched ten innings for Ep-Skenes Files with a 0.50 WHIP and an 11:1 K/BB ratio, which is the kind of line that makes scouts openly weep, and Gavin Williams gave All Betts Are Off fifteen strikeouts over twelve innings. On any other week, Skenes walks away with this award without debate — but Miller's four-trip demolition job in high-leverage situations earns him the hardware this time.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

The cream has risen, and the gap between these three and the rest of the field is starting to look like a canyon.

1. Babe Ruthless — Jordan won this league in his first year and he's running it back like he's got something to prove. A 31-16-1 record with a nine-category beatdown this week; the defending champ is not done defending.

2. Misio Soup — Best win percentage in the league at .677 and they are rolling, taking eight categories this week like they were reading from a script. Dennis has won three of these things and every year the league figures he'll cool off — every year, they're wrong.

3. All Betts Are Off — Tyler, a first-year manager, is third in the league. First year. An 8-4 win this week keeps him firmly in the conversation, and if he doesn't fall apart down the stretch, this is a legitimate title contender.

SOLID

Not in the top tier yet, but not out of the race either. These two are playing for playoff position and they both know it.

4. Ep-Skenes Files — Matt has been inconsistent all season but an 8-3 win this week is the kind of statement that buys you some goodwill. The .458 overall mark is the floor; the question is whether this week was a trend or a blip.

5. Risky Bettiness — Nate goes 7-5 this week and climbs to fifth, which feels about right for a team that has been bobbing just below the surface all season. He's in the race but he needs to stop leaving points on the table.

MEDIOCRE

Three teams in a pile, none of them inspiring confidence, all of them capable of going on a short run that means absolutely nothing if they can't sustain it.

6. Juiced Balls — Fantasy Guru drops a 5-7 week and falls to sixth, which is exactly where a .458 team with no particular identity belongs right now. There's a real player in here somewhere but he hasn't shown up for long enough to count on.

7. Professor McGonigle — Tom is the commissioner and he's a .500 man sitting at 23-23-2, which is the fantasy equivalent of a team that looks great in April and disappears in August. A 4-8 loss this week is not what a playoff hopeful needs on the ledger.

8. Jr Jays — Gordon goes 4-8 this week and slides to eighth. The roster has more talent than 20-25-3 suggests; the results just aren't showing up.

REBUILDING

These two are in the basement and the exit signs are getting harder to ignore.

9. Eephus — Jesse's first year in the league has been a rough education: 18-28-2, a 3-9 loss this week, and a win percentage that matches the weather in April — gray and uninspiring. The potential is there; the results are not.

10. Cease and Desist — Philip sits dead last at 17-29-2 and went 3-8 this week, and at some point the word "rebuilding" stops being an excuse and starts being a diagnosis. The season isn't over, but it's asking the hard questions.

Movers and Shakers

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The story of the week is Ep-Skenes Files, who vaulted four spots up the standings to sit at number five with a 20-24-4 record. Four spots. In one week. The rest of the middle tier is still figuring out what just happened to them. Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Bryan Reynolds has been waived and reclaimed in fantasy leagues across America so many times it borders on tradition, and every time managers underestimate him, he makes them pay. Babe Ruthless picked him up this week and collected 4 runs, 7 RBI, a .286 average, and a .500 OBP. That OBP is the number that stands out — Reynolds draws walks at an elite rate, and when the bat heats up, he's one of the more complete outfield options sitting on a mid-tier NL roster. Smart pickup, clean payoff.

The runners-up are worth a mention. Juiced Balls grabbed Dillon Dingler and got a .400 average, a .455 OBP, and a home run from the catcher position — the kind of production that makes the category fights a lot easier. And All Betts Are Off stashed Jakob Marsee and picked up 2 stolen bases along with a .429 OBP. Speed is always worth a look, and Marsee delivered on the one thing he was rostered to do.

Reynolds is the clear winner. Seven RBI from a waiver pickup in a single week is the kind of production that swings close matchups. Babe Ruthless saw it on the wire and pulled the trigger.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The Hall of Shame has a new exhibit, and it belongs to All Betts Are Off. Ryne Nelson threw one-third of an inning this week. One-third. He posted an ERA of 216 and a WHIP of 27. I have covered a lot of waiver moves in this league, and I have never seen a pitching line that reads like a scoreboard malfunction. Nelson got the ball, made five batters' days considerably better, and was gone. The pickup did nothing except manufacture damage to an otherwise functional ratio column. There is no spin on a 216 ERA.

Ep-Skenes Files earns a double entry this week. Yainer Diaz came aboard and hit .133 with an OBP to match — no walks, barely any contact, nothing useful across the batting categories. Ceddanne Rafaela hit .250 and contributed zero runs, zero home runs, zero RBI, and zero stolen bases. Rafaela has real tools and legitimate speed in the real world, but this particular week he delivered a line that would embarrass a Double-A roster filler. Two waiver moves from Ep-Skenes Files this week. Two entries in this column. That's efficiency of the wrong kind.

Nelson's line still tops the list. A WHIP of 27 is a career achievement in the wrong direction.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Cease and Desist went looking for pitching help this week and found considerably more than a spot start. Will Warren went 10.2 innings, struck out 17, posted a 1.69 ERA and a 0.84 WHIP, and walked nearly nobody — a K/BB ratio of 17 is not a misprint. He earned a quality start and kept working. Warren has been developing into a legitimate rotation piece for the Yankees, and this week was the argument for rostering him full-time. A streamed pitcher putting up those ratios across double-digit innings wins the pitching half of a matchup outright.

Spencer Arrighetti gave All Betts Are Off six innings, 10 strikeouts, a 1.50 ERA, and earned a quality start. Arrighetti has always had the stuff to put up lines like this, and this week he backed up the prospect pedigree. Paul Sewald delivered two clean innings and two saves-plus-holds for Ep-Skenes Files — exactly what a reliable Arizona closer option should provide.

Warren's performance stands alone. A 0.84 WHIP and 17 strikeouts from a waiver-wire stream is the kind of line that reshapes a pitching matchup entirely. Cease and Desist made the right call.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Every week has a bench blunder that demands the most attention, and this week's worst offender is Eephus. Munetaka Murakami sat on the bench for four days while posting 5 runs, 3 home runs, and 7 RBI on 6 hits. Murakami is not a fringe talent or a speculative hold — he is Japan's home run royalty, a player who chased the all-time NPB record with legitimate thunder and crossed the ocean to prove it translates. He belongs in the starting lineup every day without argument. Four days on the bench from him cost Eephus real points in real categories, and there is no explanation that covers it.

Misio Soup sends two delegates this week. Josh Naylor sat for two days and hit two home runs with 5 RBI — production that moves the HR and RBI categories directly. Konnor Griffin was planted on the bench for five full days and still posted 4 runs, 3 RBI, and 3 stolen bases in the games he played. Griffin is exactly the kind of young, fast outfielder worth starting through cold stretches because when he runs, he moves the SB column. Neither benching made sense, and this from a team that has already exhausted its entire FAAB budget and cannot go back to the wire for help.

The rest of the list is long. Risky Bettiness left Liam Hicks on the bench all week — 10 hits, a home run, 6 RBI, and a stolen base left completely untouched. Jr Jays benched Max Muncy for four days and watched two home runs and 3 RBI go to waste. And All Betts Are Off benched Michael Harris II for three days (2 HR, 4 R) while also sitting Hunter Goodman for a game in which Goodman hit two home runs and drove in two. Two home runs. In one game. On the bench. Some decisions are hard to explain, and some explain themselves.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Two trades crossed the wire this week. The more recent deal: Misio Soup sent Jacob Misiorowski to Professor McGonigle and received Logan Webb in return. Webb is one of the more dependable starters in the National League — a ground ball machine with a track record of quality starts, strong ratios, and innings that clear the league's 30 IP minimum with room to spare. Misiorowski is a young Milwaukee arm with legitimate upside, but upside and dependability are different currencies in April. Professor McGonigle is playing the ceiling game. Misio Soup cashed in for something proven. The proven arm wins this trade on paper unless Misiorowski breaks out fast.

The earlier deal is bigger and more interesting. Cease and Desist sent Brent Rooker and Maikel Garcia to Babe Ruthless in exchange for Daniel Palencia, Chase DeLauter, and Geraldo Perdomo. Three pieces for two — and the math makes it look like Cease and Desist had the leverage. They didn't. Rooker is one of the more dangerous power bats available in this format, a player who hit his way into real fantasy relevance last season and brings consistent production. Garcia adds multi-position eligibility and a real stolen base threat out of Kansas City. Babe Ruthless consolidated genuine value into two roster spots and opened a third for whatever emerges from the wire. The pieces heading to Cease and Desist are interesting — Perdomo at shortstop has been productive, DeLauter is a prospect worth watching — but the headliner went the other way. Babe Ruthless wins this one.

FAAB activity was quiet — one bid cleared the system. Ep-Skenes Files dropped $11 on Noah Schultz, releasing Jeremy Peña. Schultz is a raw arm on a White Sox team that hasn't been kind to pitching value, and Peña provides steadier categorical contributions at a more stable position. That's a questionable eleven dollars. The larger FAAB story is Misio Soup, who is sitting at zero dollars remaining in April. The full $200 starting budget is spent. That is a problem that compounds over time, and July waiver wire moves will expose it.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Professor McGonigle led the league in strikeouts by a comfortable margin — 87, against a field average of 58.1. Their pitchers earned that number. Their ERA was 6.19, worst in the league, 2.20 runs above the 3.99 field average. High strikeout rates and high ERAs can coexist when pitchers allow hard contact after missing bats all week. The Ks won't fix themselves if the underlying contact profile doesn't change.

Cease and Desist built the inverse. Six quality starts, tied for the league lead. ERA of 3.80, strikeouts at 68 — above average in both. The pitching staff is functional. The lineup posted the worst H/AB (52), R (25), HR (4), and RBI (19) in the league — every offensive counting category, dead last. At this sample size that's a roster construction issue, not a slump. The pitchers are doing their job. Someone needs to find them some run support.

Risky Bettiness hit 19 home runs this week. The league average is 9.70. Their 49 RBI led the field; the average was 32. Power numbers at nearly twice the league rate don't sustain indefinitely, but for one week the lineup was carrying the whole operation. They threw 40 innings — second-fewest in the league, 18.5 below average — and still won 7-5. When the bats are running that hot, pitching volume becomes a minor editorial comment.

Babe Ruthless owns the best record at 9-3. Their ERA is 6.01 and their K/BB is 1.53, both among the worst figures in the league. The offense — 15 HR, 44 RBI, 43 R — has been good enough to cover it week after week, but the pitching ratios are a standing bill that eventually comes due. At some point ERA catches up and the margin for error narrows. The 9-3 record is real. So is the 6.01.
