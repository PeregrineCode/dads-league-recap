Week 17 Recap (2026-07-13 — 2026-07-26)

Monday, July 27, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Cease and Desist looked cooked. Philip's crew fell behind Jordan's defending-champion Babe Ruthless by four full categories in the middle of the week, staring up at an 0-4-8 deficit with nothing to show for it. Then the switch flipped. A stretch run that flipped the board to 9-3-0 turned a laugher into a real fight, and by the weekend it had tightened all the way back to 6-6-0 on consecutive days before Cease and Desist finally slammed the door 7-4-1. Jordan's group won the closest category of the week — AVG, by a margin of .019 — and still lost the war, a reminder that champions don't get style points.

Tyler's The Bryce Is Right put an 8-4-0 beating on Risky Bettiness, but call this one a controlled demolition, not a wire-to-wire stroll. Risky Bettiness actually grabbed a 6-4-2 lead early in the week before Bryce answered with a run that opened up a 10-1-1 advantage at its widest. WHIP came down to the thinnest margin on the board, decided by 0.010, and Bryce still walked away with eight of twelve categories in a first-year manager statement win.

Fantasy Guru's Juiced Balls opened the week down 0-2-10 to Matt's Ep-Skenes Files before methodically taking over, building as much as an 8-4-0 cushion by the weekend. Ep-Skenes clawed a couple back down the stretch and the final tally settled at a tight 6-5-1, with OBP — the tightest number of the night, a 0.014 gap — going the other way to Ep-Skenes Files. Matt's squad out-homered nobody but out-worked everybody late; Fantasy Guru will take the win and not lose sleep over it.

Commissioner Tom's Professor McGonigle jumped out to an 8-1-3 lead by midweek behind a lineup hitting .288 with a 2.31 team ERA, only to watch Gordon's Jr Jays rally to a 7-5-0 edge of their own on Thursday. McGonigle's pitching held up when it mattered, and the week closed out 7-5-0, decided at the margins by a 0.020 gap in OBP. Worth noting for Gordon's group: 61 RBI and 18 homers on the board, and exactly zero stolen bases all week — somebody forgot how to run.

And then there was Dennis's Schlittler's List, which didn't just beat Jesse's Eephus, it ran the table. Twelve categories, twelve wins, not so much as a tie to soften the blow — even the tightest margin of the night, a 0.049 gap in OBP, wasn't particularly close. A rough week for Eephus in year one, and a lopsided one everywhere else in the league too.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CJ Abrams doesn't hit for this kind of power. That's the whole story here. The Nationals shortstop built his reputation on legs, not thump, but this week he turned into a wrecking ball for Professor McGonigle, going 7 HR, 15 RBI and 10 runs while hitting .487 with a .512 OBP. Rack that up in a single week and it doesn't matter what the scouting report says. Tom's got the best individual performance in the league this week, and it wasn't up for debate.

The chase pack brought real heat too. Drake Baldwin, still building his case as one of the best young catchers in the league for Schlittler's List, put up a .436 average with 5 HR and 10 RBI, the kind of week that makes it easy to forget he does his damage with catcher's gear on. Fernando Tatis Jr. did Fernando Tatis Jr. things for Juiced Balls — 3 HR, 12 RBI, 2 steals, the five-tool package that made him a franchise cornerstone in San Diego. And Jeremy Peña, an ALCS MVP with the pedigree to back it up, hit a scarcely believable .611 for Schlittler's List, which means Dennis had two guys in the top three and somehow still isn't the story of the week.

On the mound, Chris Sale reminded everybody why the Braves handed him the ball every fifth day even through all those injury-riddled years. For The Bryce Is Right, Sale threw 13 innings, struck out 17, posted a 2.08 ERA and a microscopic 0.62 WHIP with two quality starts. Tyler's got the league's best arm working right now. Right behind him, Tarik Skubal looked every bit the reigning Cy Young winner for Jr Jays, striking out 21 over 14 1/3 innings with an ERA under one. Jacob Misiorowski brought the triple-digit heat for Professor McGonigle — 18 strikeouts in just 9 innings is a swing-and-miss rate that should scare people — and Sean Burke quietly stacked 15 strikeouts and a 1.42 ERA over 12 2/3 innings for Ep-Skenes Files. Four arms, four teams, one brutal week to be facing any of them.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Contenders

Three teams have separated themselves from the pack, and folks, this is where the season gets decided. Everybody else can talk all they want — these three are playing a different game.

#1 Schlittler’s List — 122-77-5 (.610) — W, 12-0-0

A perfect week. Twelve categories, twelve wins, zero excuses. Dennis didn't just beat somebody this week, he buried them, and when you can post a 12-0-0 this deep into the season it tells me the rest of this league better start paying attention.

#2 Professor McGonigle — 128-68-8 (.647) — W, 7-5-0

Tom's got the best raw record in the entire league and he's still sitting at number two, which says everything about how stacked the top of this thing is. A 7-5-0 week is a grinder's win, not a highlight reel, but grinders are the ones holding trophies in September.

#3 The Bryce Is Right — 108-86-10 (.554) — W, 8-4-0

Tyler's a rookie and he's playing like he's been doing this for a decade. An 8-4-0 week keeps him firmly in the contender conversation, and if he keeps stacking wins like this the veterans in this league are going to have some explaining to do.

Solid

Good enough to make noise, not quite good enough to sleep easy. This tier is where the margin for error disappears.

#4 Ep-Skenes Files — 114-82-8 (.578) — L, 5-6-1

Matt's still got a strong overall record propping him up, but a 5-6-1 loss is the kind of week that starts a slide if it turns into a habit. The front office needs to figure out what went sideways before this becomes a pattern.

#5 Babe Ruthless — 104-92-8 (.529) — L, 4-7-1

The defending champ is treading water. Jordan's been here before and knows how to turn a season around, but a 4-7-1 week is not how you defend a title, and the league knows it.

Mediocre

The muddled middle. Nobody here is winning the league, but nobody's out of it either — which might be the most frustrating spot to sit in all season.

#6 Juiced Balls — 94-97-13 (.493) — W, 6-5-1

Fantasy Guru scraped out a 6-5-1 win and it's exactly the kind of unspectacular victory that keeps a season from spiraling. Nothing flashy here, just a team refusing to fall further behind.

#7 Cease and Desist — 87-107-10 (.451) — W, 7-4-1

Philip picked up a 7-4-1 win this week, and after the season he's had, I'll take it as a sign of life. It's not going to erase the record, but it's a start.

#8 Jr Jays — 94-101-9 (.483) — L, 5-7-0

Gordon's stuck in neutral. A 5-7-0 loss keeps this team hovering around .500 for the season with no real direction, and at some point neutral starts to look like a decision instead of an accident.

Rebuilding

These two are playing out the string, and the results this week did nothing to change that story.

#9 Risky Bettiness — 64-132-8 (.333) — L, 4-8-0

Another one dropped, 4-8-0, and the record now looks less like a slump and more like the season's identity. A roster on autopilot, playing out the schedule.

#10 Eephus — 61-134-9 (.321) — L, 0-12-0

Jesse got shut out across the board, 0-12-0, and there is no dressing that up. A goose egg in every category is about as bad a week as this league can produce, and in his first year that's a rough lesson to learn.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Schlittler's List ran the waiver wire like a shop with a season-ticket holder discount, and Jeremy Peña is the headliner. The Astros shortstop already has a World Series ring and an ALCS MVP trophy from 2022, but this week he played like a man trying to win the award all over again — seven runs, three home runs, three RBI, and a preposterous .611 average and .611 OBP. Four days on the roster, and he hit like the middle of the order for every one of them.

The same team doubled down with Chandler Simpson, the speedy Rays outfielder who's built his profile on legs and bat-to-ball skill rather than pop. Three runs, two RBI, two steals, and a .438 average is exactly the profile you want off waivers — the kind of add that plugs a category weakness overnight. Two burner adds from one front office in the same week is close to a monopoly.

Professor McGonigle gets an honorable mention for grabbing Steven Kwan, whose calling card has always been a bat that finds the barrel and rarely misses — a stolen base, a .333 average and .385 OBP is a quiet, professional add. It just wasn't going to outshine what Schlittler's List pulled off. This week the waiver wire had one big story, and it wore two different Astros and Rays jerseys.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jr Jays reached into the pond and pulled out Nick Gonzales, a former first-round prospect with a legitimate pedigree in Pittsburgh, and got back a stat line that could double as a rounding error — zero runs, zero homers, one RBI, zero steals. Yes, the .308 average looks fine on a business card, but an average with nothing attached to it is like ordering a steak dinner and getting handed just the steak knife.

Then there's Ep-Skenes Files, which made two questionable calls in the same week, back to back, like a front office that lost a bet. First came Erik Sabrowski, good for three innings, two strikeouts, and a 3.00 ERA that reads fine until you realize it came attached to almost nothing of substance. Then came Shane Drohan, who took the ball for six innings and left it with a 7.50 ERA and a 1.33 WHIP, numbers that belong on a highlight reel of what not to do with a bullpen slot.

Two swings, two misses in the same week is the kind of thing that gets a front office called into the owner's box. Matt's staff needs a scouting department, or at least a group text with better information in it.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Professor McGonigle found the pick of the week off the wire in Landen Roupp, and the Giants right-hander delivered exactly what a stream is supposed to deliver — six innings, seven strikeouts, a 3.00 ERA, a tidy 0.67 WHIP, and a quality start to show for it. That's a start you'd happily roll out in the middle of a playoff rotation, let alone a Tuesday waiver pickup.

The Bryce Is Right didn't walk away empty either, doubling up on streams that both delivered. Brady Singer went 6.2 innings with six strikeouts and a quality start, even if the 4.05 ERA and 1.05 WHIP were the least tidy of the bunch. Keider Montero was the better ratios play of the two — 5.1 innings, eight strikeouts, a 1.69 ERA and a microscopic 0.56 WHIP, just short of the innings needed to bank the quality start.

Roupp takes the crown for pairing dominance with the full quality start, but Tyler's front office deserves real credit for hitting on two streams in the same week. Credit a manager who did his homework before the week started.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The headline disaster belongs to Schlittler's List, who somehow benched three difference-makers in the same week. Cam Schlittler sat for a day in which he put up seven and a third innings, twelve strikeouts, and a quality start. Nathan Eovaldi sat for two days and delivered eleven innings, ten strikeouts, and a quality start of his own. And on top of all that pitching left in a drawer, Mookie Betts — yes, that Mookie Betts — sat for two days while racking up three runs, two homers, three RBI and five hits. That's a front office that accidentally benched an entire pitching staff and a future Hall of Famer in the same week and still swept the week anyway.

The Bryce Is Right and Eephus both have longer-running issues to answer for. Tyler left Hunter Goodman on the bench for four separate days of production — three runs, three home runs, eight RBI, five hits — a lineup-setting habit that needs breaking, not a one-time oversight. Jesse did the same with Munetaka Murakami, benching him for four days worth of four runs, two homers, six RBI and five hits. Two teams, two power bats, parked for the better part of a week each.

Smaller but still costly: Juiced Balls sat Drew Rasmussen for a day that produced seven innings, nine strikeouts and a quality start, and Professor McGonigle — fresh off winning Stream of the Week — benched Hunter Brown for a start that went seven innings, seven strikeouts and a quality start of his own. Nobody's perfect, not even the guy who just won Stream of the Week.

The Insider Report

Sources tell me the tension at the top of Dad's Baseball League 9.0 has a name, and it belongs to Dennis. Schlittler's List sits second at 122-77-5, with Ep-Skenes Files right behind at 114-82-8, and multiple people close to the roster describe a front office that's suddenly playing defense instead of offense. That's what happens when you're getting squeezed from both directions — Professor McGonigle locked in above him at the top, Matt's club climbing from below. Two threats at once tends to make a manager nervous, and nervous managers start making calls.

I'm told nothing on the Schlittler's List roster should be considered untouchable right now. That's a notable shift in posture for a team that's spent most of the season looking comfortable in the top tier. Whether that means Dennis is shopping for an upgrade to pull away from Matt, or bracing to sell off a piece if the gap keeps closing, depends on who you ask. Either way, keep an eye on the trade block — a front office willing to move anybody is a front office that's about to make noise.

The more interesting question is who Matt calls first. Ep-Skenes Files has the form to keep pressing — third place, a winning record, clearly built to climb rather than coast. A move that widens the gap on Schlittler's List would do more than settle a two-team staring contest; it would reshuffle the entire top of the standings heading into the stretch run before the August 6 deadline.

Meanwhile, the middle of the pack has to be watching this with interest. The Bryce Is Right and Babe Ruthless are close enough to the contenders that any deal shaking up the top three could open a lane, and the mediocre cluster behind them — Juiced Balls, Jr Jays, Cease and Desist — are all bunched tight in the standings with plenty of season left to decide whether they buy in or bail. If Dennis is picking up the phone, don't be surprised if it starts a chain reaction. That's how it usually goes around here.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Jr Jays led the league in innings pitched (96.2) and quality starts (9), and shared the league lead in RBI (61) with Babe Ruthless. None of it mattered. Professor McGonigle needed only 58.1 innings and a single quality start, the fewest in the league, to take the matchup 7-5-0, winning seven categories on the back of a 2.31 ERA, a 0.99 WHIP and a league-best 12 saves-plus-holds. Jr Jays out-scored, out-struck-out and out-threw their opponent and still lost. Counting stats only count if enough of them add up to a category win.

Babe Ruthless ran into the same problem from the other direction. Twenty home runs, tops in the league, and a share of the RBI lead against a league average of 45.5 did nothing to prevent a 4-7-1 loss to Cease and Desist. The lineup turned in a .285 OBP while the pitching staff posted a 1.39 WHIP and a 2.50 K/BB, all three the worst marks in the league, and Cease and Desist cleaned up strikeouts, ERA, WHIP, K/BB and saves-plus-holds on the way to seven category wins. Twenty homers bought exactly four categories.

Then there is Schlittler's List, 12-0-0 over Eephus, a full sweep of the board. The staff threw 61.2 innings, well under the league average of 72.9, and still posted a 2.48 ERA to go with a league-best 0.84 WHIP and a league-best 6.82 K/BB, backed by a league-best .293 average and .356 OBP. Four league-leading rate stats carried every single category, innings floor be damned.

Three matchups, one pattern. Rate stats travel across every category they touch. Counting stats only travel as far as the categories they happen to win. And a quality start total that would embarrass most fantasy managers is no obstacle at all when the ERA, WHIP and K/BB columns are already locked down.
