Weekly Recap

Week 8 Recap (2026-05-11 — 2026-05-17)

Monday, May 18, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Tuesday night was where this one turned. Through two days of play, All Betts Are Off sat three categories to nine, trailing Risky Bettiness by six with the week very much alive. Then Wednesday's games settled and the board had flipped — All Betts Are Off led six to five. They never looked back. The final: an 8-3-1 drubbing built on a pitching staff that posted a 0.82 ERA and 0.91 WHIP for the week. A rotation locking down runs at that clip turns six-category deficits into survivable starting points. Risky Bettiness outpaced their opponent in RBI (32 to 25), matched them homer-for-homer (ten each), and edged the OBP category by 0.006. Three category wins doesn't move the needle against a 0.82 ERA — the matchup turned on pitching, and the pitching wasn't close.

Babe Ruthless and Cease and Desist staged the week's most twisting matchup, with two lead changes before it was settled. Through Monday, Babe Ruthless led ten categories to one — the kind of opening-day statement that makes opponents think twice. Cease and Desist stormed back through the middle of the week and held the lead by as many as two categories heading into the final days. Babe Ruthless flipped it back late and held on for the 7-4-1 win. The difference was quality starts, six to two. Cease and Desist won ERA (3.75 to 4.02) and average (.216 to .194), but six quality starts against two is a category gap with mass.

Ep-Skenes Files needed exactly one day to make this one unwatchable. Led Juiced Balls nine categories to one on opening night, went ten to zero by Tuesday, and coasted to the 8-4-0 final. The offense carried it: 40 runs, 12 homers, 43 RBI, 10 stolen bases. Juiced Balls actually had the cleaner pitching numbers — 3.30 ERA, 1.10 WHIP, seven quality starts — and it bought them four categories and nothing else. Wire-to-wire, no drama, no suspense.

Professor McGonigle did not show up to play around, posting a 1.60 ERA, 0.99 WHIP, 72 strikeouts, and 11 saves-and-holds on the way to an 8-4-0 win over Jr Jays. Jr Jays hit 12 home runs, scored 39 runs, and won four categories outright — a legitimate offensive week, played against a pitching performance that made it irrelevant. No offense in the league talks its way out of a 1.60 ERA. Finally, Kirby's Dream Team handed Eephus a 10-2-0 loss, the week's most comprehensive result. The batting line read .298 average, .366 OBP, ten stolen bases, ten home runs. Eephus held ERA and SV+H and lost everything else. Sometimes one team is simply operating at a different altitude.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Corbin Carroll put up a .421 average with a .607 on-base percentage and eight runs scored — this from the guy who won NL Rookie of the Year in 2023 and has been looking for a full return to that form ever since. Ep-Skenes Files got every version of Carroll this week: the table-setter, the base-stealer, the guy who grinds out at-bats deep into counts. Two home runs, two stolen bases, and a line that would make Arizona's front office feel very good about the extension conversation. When Carroll is locked in like this, he's one of the five most valuable outfielders in the game. This week, he was exactly that.

The runners-up deserve their flowers too. Nick Kurtz went and posted nine RBI with three home runs for Cease and Desist — the fourth overall pick in 2024 is hitting like a top-of-the-draft talent. Randy Arozarena delivered a .423 average with three stolen bags for Kirby's Dream Team, reminding everyone that the 2020 ALCS folk hero still has enough gas left to wreck a fantasy week. Kyle Schwarber slugged four home runs and drove in nine for Risky Bettiness, which is exactly what Schwarber does when the fastballs find the middle of the plate.

Zack Wheeler wins Pitcher of the Week and the rest of the field couldn't get into the same area code. Fourteen-plus innings, a 0.63 ERA, 0.77 WHIP, a 12-to-1 strikeout-to-walk ratio, two quality starts — Juiced Balls owned every pitching category Wheeler touched this week. This is the Wheeler who's been a legitimate Cy Young contender for three years running and has never quite gotten the national recognition he deserves. Cade Smith poked his head in with seven strikeouts and a 0.30 WHIP in 3.1 innings for Professor McGonigle — the Cleveland bullpen piece is filthy right now and worth watching. Joe Ryan and Nathan Eovaldi gave quality starts for Risky Bettiness and Kirby's Dream Team respectively, keeping the category totals ticking, but Wheeler was the story this week.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The Contenders

Three teams are playing a different sport than the rest of this league, and the gap is getting uglier by the week.

#1 All Betts Are Off — The 8-3 win keeps them at the top of the mountain, and at 60-34-2, the most category wins in the league are hard to argue with. Tyler's franchise has gone from first-year novelty to genuine threat, and the rest of the contenders know it.

#2 Professor McGonigle — The highest win percentage in the league (.641) but sitting second because Tom dropped a category in the standings count and The Hammer doesn't reward technicalities. Still, 59-32-5 with an 8-4 week is a statement — McGonigle is the engine that keeps running even when the rest of the field starts sputtering.

#3 Kirby's Dream Team — Ten categories in a single week. Dennis's squad didn't just win, it dismantled — the 10-2 result is the best performance by any team this week and the kind of showing that reshuffles the contender conversation. A few more weeks like that and this ranking gets revisited.

Solid

These two are playoff-caliber and dangerous, but they haven't proven they can run with the top tier yet.

#4 Babe Ruthless — Jordan came into this season with a championship pedigree and he's playing like it. A 7-4 week keeps him comfortably above .500 and in the thick of things. The defending champ hasn't separated from the pack below him, but he hasn't needed to yet.

#5 Ep-Skenes Files — Matt's crew goes 8-4 and climbs to exactly .516 on the season — the definition of a team that wins the weeks it's supposed to win and nothing more. The pitching is there when the staff shows up. Consistency is the only question.

Mediocre

Mathematically alive, spiritually adrift.

#6 Jr Jays — Gordon's team drops a 4-8 week and slides to 41-48-7 — that's a team spending more time below .500 than above it. The category balance isn't there, and the losses are piling up in the wrong places.

#7 Juiced Balls — Fantasy Guru has been outplayed by his own reputation this season. Four categories in an 8-category week is the calling card of a roster that looks good on paper and performs adequately in practice. That gap has to close.

#8 Risky Bettiness — Nate's 3-8 week is the kind of result that keeps you up at night. At 35-54-7, this team is dangerously close to the rebuilding tier whether it wants to admit it or not. Something has to change.

Rebuilding

The math is not a wall yet, but the window is closing fast.

#9 Eephus — Jesse goes 2-10. Two. The rookie campaign has been a rough education, and a 36-56-4 record with the worst week in the league is a tough spot to claw back from. Jesse's got time to figure it out — just not as much time as he had a month ago.

#10 Cease and Desist — Philip's 32-59-5 record has The Hammer's full attention — and not the good kind. The 4-7 week offers a glimmer, but this team has been sitting at the bottom of the standings long enough that it's starting to look permanent. The season isn't over. It just looks that way from here.

Movers and Shakers

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The bottom of the standings rearranged itself this week, and calling it a "mover" feels generous. Risky Bettiness climbed one spot to eighth place with a 35-54-7 mark, nudging past Eephus, which slid to ninth at 36-56-4. One team moved up. One team moved down. Neither should be throwing a parade.

What's worth noting is that Eephus actually holds the edge in outright wins and still sits below Risky Bettiness in the standings — which tells you everything about how ties have punished that club all season. Four ties to Risky Bettiness's seven is the kind of stat-line difference that shows up in the standings like a slow leak. In a category-based format, ties are the currency of the perpetually frustrated, and Eephus is running a surplus.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Brayan Rocchio slashed .667/.750 for Juiced Balls this week, added a home run and a pair of runs scored, and gave the front office everything a manager could ask for from a waiver wire move. The sample is small — it always is with these pickups — but Rocchio is a legitimate everyday shortstop for Cleveland, a guy who barrels the ball consistently when he's rolling. Juiced Balls read the wire right and got rewarded.

The same front office doubled up with Luke Raley out of Seattle, who contributed a stolen base and a .375 OBP as a secondary add. Eephus also gets a mention for claiming Alec Bohm out of Philadelphia, who posted a .313 average with a pair of runs scored at the corner infield spot. Decent value across the board, but when someone's posting .667 on the week, the award isn't much of a debate.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Josh Jung stepped to the plate repeatedly for Juiced Balls and produced a .077 OBP — one walk in the entire week, no runs, no power. Jung has been one of the most injury-plagued players in Texas over the past two seasons, the wrist a recurring problem that shows up in the swing, and picking him up right now requires either deep optimism or a very short memory. The same front office that wins the best pickup award this week also takes the top Hall of Shame slot.

Eephus took a flier on Carter Jensen behind the plate in Kansas City and got .143 AVG, .143 OBP, and a roster spot that could have been used on basically anyone else. Jensen is a young catcher working through the developmental process — fine in reality, costly in fantasy. Then there's Pete Fairbanks, claimed by Kirby's Dream Team. Fairbanks was a legitimate late-inning weapon in Tampa Bay, triple-digit fastball and all, and this week in Miami he delivered one inning, two strikeouts, no saves, no holds, and a 2.00 WHIP. The Tampa closer era is over.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ryan Weathers went 6.1 innings for Ep-Skenes Files, struck out nine hitters, posted a 0.63 WHIP, and earned a quality start — about as complete a streaming performance as the waiver wire delivers. Weathers has been developing into a legitimate rotation piece in New York after some mixed results earlier in his career, and this was the kind of outing where everything was working: command, fastball life, the secondary stuff keeping hitters off balance. Ep-Skenes Files identified the spot start and ran it perfectly.

Casey Mize put up a 0.00 ERA and 0.33 WHIP across six innings for Jr Jays — a nearly untouchable line — but only four strikeouts limit the fantasy return on what was otherwise a gem. Mize has been battling back from Tommy John for years, and a clean outing like that is genuinely encouraging for Detroit's rotation picture. Peter Lambert gave Babe Ruthless seven innings, a 6:1 K/BB, and a quality start out of the Houston rotation. All three managers made the right call. Weathers takes the week on strikeout volume.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Start at the top. Kirby's Dream Team acquired Kyle Bradish via trade on May 12th, then left him on the bench for the entire week while he went out and threw six innings, seven strikeouts, and a quality start. Bradish is a legitimate top-of-rotation arm for Baltimore when healthy — a manager acquires a pitcher like that specifically to win pitching categories — and Kirby's Dream Team watched the whole performance from the pine. The front office will need to explain that one at the next league meeting.

Cease and Desist benched Bryan Woo on the day he took the mound and produced six innings, nine strikeouts, and a quality start. Woo is one of the more talented young arms in Seattle, and nine K's sitting on the bench is the kind of stat line that stings at season's end. Risky Bettiness sat Cade Cavalli the entire week — 6.1 innings, eight K's, a quality start — and All Betts Are Off left both Gavin Williams (seven K's, quality start) and Sandy Alcantara (six K's, quality start) out of the rotation on their respective start days. Alcantara won a Cy Young Award in his prime, pitched a legitimate quality start, and All Betts Are Off scored zero points from it.

On the hitting side, Ep-Skenes Files planted Christian Walker on the bench for six of his game days while Walker hit two home runs, drove in three, and scored three times. Walker is a proven power bat and that production mattered. Between the pitching and the hitting, this was a rough week for managers who trust their bench settings.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

One trade crossed the wire this week: Babe Ruthless landed Jarren Duran from Kirby's Dream Team in exchange for Kyle Bradish, and both sides acquired a piece worth talking about. Bradish is an Orioles starter with legitimate front-of-rotation upside when healthy — the kind of arm that can swing five pitching categories on a good week. The injury history is real, but so is the ceiling, and Kirby's Dream Team clearly identified rotation reinforcement as the priority heading into the second month.

The edge goes to Babe Ruthless. Duran is exactly what a categories league requires from an outfielder — speed, contact, runs, and the kind of consistent production that a Boston lineup supports night after night. He's durable, he contributes across multiple categories every week, and his fantasy floor is considerably higher than Bradish's. One footnote: Kirby's Dream Team acquired Bradish on May 12th, then benched him for the entire first week they owned him. Somewhere in Baltimore, Bradish pitched a quality start and had no fantasy idea where the points went.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Kirby's Dream Team batted .298 with a .366 OBP and led the league in hits (72) while tying Ep-Skenes Files for the lead in stolen bases (10). They also posted the worst ERA in the league at 4.67, against a league average of 3.49. That combination shouldn't sustain a 10-2-0 record, and yet here we are. The batting is doing enough heavy lifting to drag the pitching across the finish line. A 4.67 ERA is a debt that compounds, and eventually the schedule will present a week where the offense can't cover it.

Jr Jays tied Ep-Skenes Files for the league lead in home runs (12 each) and finished second in runs scored (39). They also hit .183 — the worst average in the league — with just 39 hits, the fewest of any team. In their matchup against Professor McGonigle, all 12 of those home runs bought them exactly three category wins. The league's HR leader going 3-for-12 is a useful data point: in a 12-category format, power alone doesn't win weeks. The categories you forfeit hitting for the fences have to come from somewhere.

The most efficient misuse of good pitching belongs to Juiced Balls. Seven quality starts — best in the league. K/BB of 4.07, best in the league against a 3.18 average. Seventy-one innings, also the most. Their ERA was 3.30, WHIP was 1.10. Then their lineup produced 22 runs and 16 RBI, both league-worst marks, and Ep-Skenes Files won eight of twelve categories. When your starters do everything right and your offense can't clear the league average in a single batting category, the box score starts to look like a math problem with no good answer.
