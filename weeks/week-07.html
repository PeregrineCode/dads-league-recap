Week 7 Recap (2026-05-04 — 2026-05-10)

Monday, May 11, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Professor McGonigle hung an 11-0-1 final on Cease and Desist that demands a second look. The staff posted a 1.22 ERA, a 0.77 WHIP, a 6.92 K/BB ratio, and 90 strikeouts. The lone tie was quality starts — five each — because when the ERA is 1.22, individual outings aren't where things collapse. Wire to wire, no close calls, one hand on the wheel all seven days. Post that on the scoreboard and say nothing.

Then there was Kirby's Dream Team, who apparently needed six days to find the gear. After losing the lead on Day 2, Babe Ruthless held control straight through Saturday — sitting on a five-category lead with a 2.14 ERA and a 1.12 WHIP to back it up. But Kirby's Dream Team had been grinding in strikeouts (63 to 49), K/BB (3.94 to 2.23), and quality starts (5 to 3) all week, and on Sunday the accumulated edge finally flipped the board. Final: 7-5-0. Closest category was AVG at a margin of .013, which tells exactly how tight the grip was right to the end. Two lead changes, a five-category deficit overcome, a win on the final day — that's the week in one matchup.

Ep-Skenes Files had All Betts Are Off charted from the opening bell — a 10-0-2 Day 1 snapshot that announced the week's destination early. The final 9-3-0 just confirmed what Monday already said. Eighty-two strikeouts, 3.10 ERA, 1.03 WHIP, 5 quality starts, 8 SV+H. All Betts Are Off posted a 5.89 ERA and zero saves-plus-holds — a difficult combination in a 12-category format — and held the edge only in R, SB, and OBP. Those three categories kept it off the historical-blowout shortlist. Everything else ran in one direction all week.

The Jr Jays and Juiced Balls matchup flipped twice before settling. Juiced Balls grabbed the lead on Day 2, Jr Jays reclaimed it on Day 3, and it stayed there through Sunday's 7-3-2 final. Here's the line that defines this matchup: Juiced Balls hit one home run on the week. One, across six days of baseball. Their batting line read .175 AVG and 18 RBI while Jr Jays posted a 1.64 ERA and 0.91 WHIP. Juiced Balls won K, K/BB, and SV+H — the arms held up — but the offense left the building, and in a 12-category format that math always catches up.

Finally, a tie — and a legitimately contested one. Risky Bettiness and Eephus split at 5-5-2, OBP the nearest miss at a margin of .023. Risky Bettiness posted 16 home runs and took the contact side of the ledger; Eephus answered with 39 strikeouts and 5 saves-plus-holds, built on pitching volume — 43⅔ innings to Risky Bettiness's 30. Five categories apiece, two tied. Half a point for each side. Someone walked away from that week standing completely still, and they ought to be absolutely furious about it.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Andy Pages put on a full offensive demonstration this week, and the Ep-Skenes Files are entitled to brag. Four home runs, eight RBI, a .417 batting average, a .440 OBP — Pages punished mistakes, took his walks, and made pitchers earn every out. The Dodger outfielder has always had the power ceiling for a week like this. The plate discipline showing up at the same time is what turned it into a runaway.

The runners-up are all worth the camera time. Bobby Witt Jr. scored seven runs and stole two bags for Jr Jays, doing what Bobby Witt Jr. does — all of it, at once, at full speed. Bryce Harper posted a .520 OBP for All Betts Are Off, the kind of one-week burst only a handful of hitters on the planet can produce. Josh Naylor piled up three stolen bases at a .458 clip for Kirby's Dream Team — three steals, from a first baseman — and that alone earns the mention.

Cristopher Sánchez takes the pitching award for Professor McGonigle, and the line speaks for itself: 15 innings, 17 strikeouts, one walk, a 0.67 WHIP, two quality starts. Sánchez has developed into one of the premier command artists in the National League, a southpaw who treats the strike zone like real estate and charges rent on every inch. Davis Martin deserves recognition for Jr Jays — 19 strikeouts in 13 innings, a 0.69 ERA, a pitcher who keeps insisting he belongs at this level. Jacob deGrom's 17 strikeouts for Juiced Balls, delivered with a 17 K/BB ratio, served notice that when deGrom is healthy and the slider is working, the ceiling on that arm is still stratospheric.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

Three teams are pulling away from the field, and the gap is growing every week.

1. Professor McGonigle — An 11-0-1 demolition is the kind of week that reverberates through future matchups. Fifty-one wins before June is a pace that makes everyone else's playoff math uncomfortable.

2. All Betts Are Off — A 3-9 loss would be cause for alarm if this team weren't sitting on 52 wins — the best raw total in the league. One rough week from the top-record team is a speed bump, and that's all it is.

3. Kirby's Dream Team — The 7-5 win was steady rather than spectacular, and steady is enough to hold tier-one status. Two championships in 2022 and 2023, and a .565 record that says the franchise hasn't forgotten how to compete.

SOLID

Not contenders yet, but both teams won this week and closed the gap on the teams above them.

4. Ep-Skenes Files — The 9-3 win was the most dominant performance outside the top three this week, and it earns this ranking over Babe Ruthless despite the inferior record. Trajectory matters as much as the standings line, and Ep-Skenes Files is pointed upward.

5. Jr Jays — A 7-3 win keeps Jr Jays on the right side of .500 and in the playoff picture. Not a flashy operation, not a scary one — just a team that keeps finding ways to win.

MEDIOCRE

The middle of this league is a logjam, and everyone here knows they're one bad week from the basement conversation.

6. Babe Ruthless — The best record in this tier at .524, but the 5-7 loss this week slides them down a peg. Last year's champion has been drifting for weeks now, and defending champions don't drift their way back to the top.

7. Juiced Balls — The 3-7 loss is the kind of result that sends a manager staring at the roster wondering what happened to preseason optimism. Below .500 and losing ground; something has to change.

8. Eephus — The 5-5 tie is the most unsatisfying outcome in sports, and Eephus has collected too many of them in year one. Forty-six losses in the column before June; the math is not cooperating with the effort.

REBUILDING

Two teams are running out of calendar, and both of them know it.

9. Risky Bettiness — Another 5-5 tie, another week of spinning wheels. The 32-46-6 record has a slow-motion quality to it — each week blurring into the next without moving the needle.

10. Cease and Desist — An 0-11-1 week is the brutal mirror image of first place's romp through them, and the symmetry is merciless. A .357 winning percentage with weeks left to play, and the losing streak has hardened from a rough patch into a season-defining trend.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ryan Jeffers put up a .353 average and a .450 OBP from the catcher spot, and Jr Jays deserves full credit for making the move. Four runs, a homer, and an on-base percentage that would look good from any position in the lineup — from a catcher, it's a genuine weekly advantage. The runners-up were both pulled by Eephus, which had an active and productive week in the front office. Two useful adds from one roster in a single week. That's front office work worth acknowledging.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Let's start with Brendan Donovan, because Cease and Desist deserves an explanation for what happened there. A .100 average and a .100 on-base percentage. One hit in ten at-bats, zero walks, zero extra bases, nothing in the counting categories. Donovan has bounced around and landed in Seattle this year without finding a role, and this add cost a roster move for a line that you'd accept from a pitcher. The multi-position eligibility was the sell — the production was nowhere.

Juiced Balls took a swing on Mickey Moniak in Colorado, which is a reasonable instinct — the altitude helps hitters who put the ball in play. Moniak did not put the ball in play at a useful rate, posting a .182 average with a .250 OBP that arrived entirely through walks. The Coors bump requires contact to activate. Meanwhile, Ep-Skenes Files brought in Ildemaro Vargas from Arizona for one RBI and a .222/.222 slash line — no walks drawn, no extra-base hits, and an OBP that matched his batting average exactly, which tells you everything. Vargas is a utility option for a team in a bind. Ep-Skenes Files appears to have been in a bind this week.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Mitch Keller gave Jr Jays a clean, efficient line from the streaming spot — six innings, a quality start, a 1.00 WHIP, a 3.00 ERA, and a 2.00 K/BB ratio. Four strikeouts won't move the needle in that category, but Keller went six deep without giving the game back, which is exactly what you're asking a streamed arm to do. Pittsburgh's rotation has stabilized around him as a reliable mid-rotation presence, and the Jr Jays front office clearly did their homework on the matchup.

Smart streaming is about identifying the right arm for the right week and not overthinking it. In a week when some managers were pulling levers frantically with nothing to show for it, this was a composed, calculated pickup that produced a start worth having. One quality start, one contribution to ERA and WHIP, one move that paid off. That's the job.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Risky Bettiness benched Tanner Bibee for an entire week. The entire week. Bibee went out and threw ten innings, struck out thirteen batters, and earned a quality start — all from the bench. Thirteen strikeouts sitting unused while Risky Bettiness ran whatever was active in his rotation. Bibee has been one of Cleveland's more dependable starters, a guy you put in your lineup and trust. Apparently that trust has its limits.

If that were the only item on the docket, it would be enough. But Liam Hicks also spent the full week on the bench, went seven-for-AB with two home runs and six RBI, and contributed nothing to Risky Bettiness because he was never activated. Two players, one week, both left completely idle while producing. The roster management here requires a formal review.

The rest of the league's ledger is tame by comparison, though not spotless. Eephus kept Jo Adell on the bench for three of his active days while Adell hit two home runs and drove in a pair. Jr Jays sat Xander Bogaerts through four of his active days — a homer, three RBI, and a stolen base left on the table. Cease and Desist benched Tyler Soderstrom for two days that included a home run. Juiced Balls let Corey Seager sit for one day where he homered and knocked in two. Add it all up and it was a rough week for active lineup management across the board. But Risky Bettiness set the standard for what not to do, and the standard was set high.

The club is hovering at sixth place, too good to tank and not quite good enough to chase down the top two. And the rumour I've been sitting on all week: Professor McGonigle, the league leader, is shopping Elly De La Cruz. Sitting at 51-28-5 and moving De La Cruz takes conviction — or a very specific vision for what comes back. My read is a pitching consolidation play, and a team like All Betts Are Off or Kirby's Dream Team could be the landing spot if the price lines up. The deadline is August 6th. The deals that matter get made before the panic sets in. DiNapoli out.

Ones That Got Away

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

While the rest of the league was squabbling over the obvious names, a few managers were quietly cashing in on the bargain bin. Risky Bettiness had Matthew Liberatore working a quality start at 9% owned — six innings, 1.50 ERA, a 1.00 WHIP, and six strikeouts out of a Cardinals rotation that nobody respects. Nine percent. You could find a more crowded waiver wire in a rookie league. Professor McGonigle meanwhile had Erik Sabrowski posting a 0.38 WHIP and a K/BB of 5 out of the CLE bullpen. The man was practically invisible on the wire and delivering elite ratios.

Ep-Skenes Files played both sides of the NL Central bullpens, stashing JoJo Romero out of St. Louis and Graham Ashcraft out of Cincinnati and squeezing a combined four SV+H out of the pair. Romero at 13% owned, Ashcraft at 14% — both delivering in the holds game while the rest of the league burned FAAB dollars chasing closers with name recognition. This is what separates the managers who work the waiver wire from the ones who just skim it. The numbers were there for anyone paying attention.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Professor McGonigle posted a K/BB of 6.92 this week. The league average was 3.57. That gap — nearly double — is the kind of number that looks like a data error until you verify it twice and realize it's just dominant pitching. The rest of the line confirms it: ERA 1.22 against a league average of 3.75, WHIP 0.77 against a league average of 1.14. They took five of six pitching categories — quality starts tied at five each — and are now 11-0-1. The K/BB ratio is the cause; the record is the result.

All Betts Are Off led the league in runs this week with 39 and posted the second-best OBP at .351. They won three batting categories against Ep-Skenes Files. Then the pitching happened: ERA 5.89 (worst in the league), WHIP 1.49 (worst in the league), zero saves or holds all week. They lost all six pitching categories and the matchup 3-9. The offense was doing its job; the pitching gave back everything and then some.

The strangest line of the week belongs to Juiced Balls: 68 strikeouts on the week, five quality starts (tied for the league high), a K/BB of 4.00 against a league average of 3.57. Those peripherals point toward a functional, reasonably command-oriented rotation. The ERA of 5.46 disagrees sharply. For what it's worth, the .175 team batting average and one home run for the entire week explain the 3-7-2 loss to Jr Jays more than anything that happened on the mound — the pitching stats offered a plausible excuse, and the batting stats quietly removed it.
