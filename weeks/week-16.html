Week 16 Recap (2026-07-06 — 2026-07-12)

Monday, July 13, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Schlittler's List and Babe Ruthless treated this league to the closest thing to a heavyweight fight we've seen all year. Dead even at 5-5-2 through Monday. Babe Ruthless then seized control, building a three-category cushion at 7-4-1 that held through Wednesday, before Dennis's crew clawed level again on Thursday. Babe Ruthless answered right back, up 6-5-1 Friday, and it looked like the week was theirs to lose. Then the weekend hit and Schlittler's List flipped the entire script, surging to an 8-4-0 lead Saturday and holding on for the 7-5-0 final — with the deciding category, AVG, settled by three thousandths of a point, .267 to .264. A twelve-round war of attrition, and Dennis landed the last punch.

Jr Jays throttled Juiced Balls 7-5-0 behind an offense that looked like it raided the batting cage all week — a .316 team average, 46 runs, 46 RBI. Gordon's group took five of the six batting categories — only steals got away — and grabbed strikeouts and quality starts too, surviving OBP by just .068 for the tightest margin of the matchup. Fantasy Guru's staff didn't embarrass itself either — a 2.49 ERA, a 0.95 WHIP and seven saves-plus-holds is a genuinely strong pitching week — but five categories isn't enough when the other dugout is hitting like that.

Risky Bettiness edged Eephus 7-4-1 in the tightest finish on the board, with AVG splitting the difference by four thousandths, .235 to .231. Nate's staff posted a 6.88 ERA that would get pulled off sports radio, yet somehow still banked WHIP, K/BB and saves-plus-holds. Jesse's group grabbed home runs, RBI and strikeouts and the two sides tied on quality starts, but it wasn't enough — Risky Bettiness took seven categories to Eephus's four.

Professor McGonigle put on an absolute clinic against Cease and Desist, 11-1-0, surrendering nothing but runs scored. Tom's roster racked up 11 saves-plus-holds alone while sweeping homers, RBI, steals, both averages and the entire pitching ledger. Philip's only consolation was runs, 33 to 28, and even the tightest category, OBP, wasn't remotely close at a .055 gap.

Ep-Skenes Files ran through The Bryce Is Right just as violently, 11-1-0, with Matt's lineup piling up 44 runs and a .351 OBP behind a 2.45 ERA and 0.93 WHIP that would headline any real rotation. Tyler's lone win came in K/BB, 3.80 to 3.71, and that was the whole story of his week. Everywhere else it was a laugher, with the closest remaining category, AVG, decided by ten points, .217 to .207.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

James Wood turned in the kind of week that makes you remember why the Nationals fleeced San Diego for him in the first place. The former centerpiece of the Juan Soto deal went full five-tool monster for Jr Jays, posting 11 runs, five homers, eight RBI and two steals while hitting .500 with a .655 OBP. Every single at-bat felt like an event — pitchers simply stopped finding the barrel. Wood has always had the raw power and speed to be special; this week he was unhittable, un-pitch-around-able, and unfair to whoever had to face Jr Jays in the batting categories.

Right behind him, Ben Rice kept his breakout rolling for Babe Ruthless, going deep five times with 12 RBI and a .423 average — a Yankees lineup spot that keeps paying off for Jordan. Esmerlyn Valdez chipped in four bombs and ten RBI for Ep-Skenes Files, while Jordan Walker reminded everyone in Schlittler's List why he was a top prospect not long ago, mixing eight runs with a pair of steals and a .321 average.

On the mound, nobody touched Zack Wheeler. The Phillies ace threw 13 innings across two starts for Juiced Balls, struck out 24 batters, walked almost nobody, and finished with a 0.69 ERA and a 0.62 WHIP. Two quality starts, a 12.0 K/BB, the whole package — this is the same Wheeler who has been a Cy Young threat for half a decade, and Fantasy Guru got the full ace treatment. Hunter Greene and Cam Schlittler both turned in strong weeks for Schlittler's List, with Greene striking out 12 in seven innings and Schlittler going 14 and two-thirds with a 1.84 ERA, while Gavin Williams gave The Bryce Is Right a sharp 2.57 ERA outing with 11 strikeouts. Good weeks, all of them — just not Wheeler's league.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Contenders

The top of this league has separated itself from the pack, and it's time we start calling this a three-team race for the trophy.

#1 Professor McGonigle — 121-63-8 (.651) — W, 11-1-0

Tom runs this league and apparently runs the standings too. An 11-1-0 beatdown this week pushes the commissioner's squad to the best record in Dad's Baseball League 9.0, and nobody else is within shouting distance.

#2 Ep-Skenes Files — 109-76-7 (.586) — W, 11-1-0

Matt matched McGonigle category for category with his own 11-1-0 demolition. Two teams posted the exact same lopsided score in the same week, and the rest of the league should read it as a message: the ceiling just got higher.

#3 Schlittler's List — 110-77-5 (.586) — W, 7-5-0

Dennis kept pace with a quieter 7-5-0 win, sitting right there at .586 alongside Ep-Skenes Files. No fireworks this week, just steady business from a team that knows how to close.

Solid

Behind the big three sits a tier of teams with real playoff cases, even if the record doesn't scream contender yet.

#4 Babe Ruthless — 100-85-7 (.539) — L, 5-7-0

The defending champ stumbled 5-7-0 this week, and Jordan knows better than anyone that a title defense has room for a bad Tuesday. Still comfortably above .500, but the margin for error just got thinner.

#5 Jr Jays — 89-94-9 (.487) — W, 7-5-0

Gordon's crew is still under water on the season but banked a 7-5-0 win to start climbing out. Nine ties in the record book is a strange fingerprint for a team that just showed it can win outright.

Mediocre

This is the muddiest tier in the league — one team is treading water, and two just got run off the field by identical 1-11-0 scores.

#6 Juiced Balls — 88-92-12 (.490) — L, 5-7-0

Fantasy Guru's team lives in category purgatory with a league-high pile of ties, and a 5-7-0 loss this week didn't do anything to break the pattern. Hovering right around .500 is exactly where this roster seems destined to stay.

#7 The Bryce Is Right — 100-82-10 (.547) — L, 1-11-0

Tyler's overall number, .547, would put this team in the Solid tier without blinking. But a 1-11-0 collapse this week is the kind of outing that gets a manager's phone blowing up with concerned texts, and it's dragging the ranking down with it.

#8 Cease and Desist — 80-103-9 (.440) — L, 1-11-0

Philip matched Tyler's disaster with his own 1-11-0 loss, and unlike The Bryce Is Right, there's no cushion of a good overall record to hide behind. Back-to-back tiers below where this roster's talent probably deserves.

Rebuilding

The basement is the basement, but at least one team down here found a reason to smile this week.

#9 Risky Bettiness — 60-124-8 (.333) — W, 7-4-1

Nate finally got one, taking down an opponent 7-4-1 after a season that's been mostly rubble. One win doesn't rebuild a .333 record, but it's proof the roster still has a pulse.

#10 Eephus — 61-122-9 (.341) — L, 4-7-1

Jesse's rookie season in this league continues to be a grind, with a 4-7-1 loss keeping the team parked at the bottom. There's no shame in a tough first year, but the standings don't hand out participation trophies.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ep-Skenes Files hit the waiver wire like Matt actually does his homework this time. Jake Bauers, the well-traveled corner bat who's bounced from Tampa to Cleveland to Milwaukee over the years, doesn't have a superstar's name attached to him, but he played like one all week: five runs, two homers, five RBI, a steal, and a .292 average with a .469 OBP that would make any leadoff man jealous. That's a complete stat line from a guy most managers wouldn't have looked at twice.

Philip wasn't sitting on his hands either. Cease and Desist grabbed both Luke Keaschall and Dominic Canzone off the wire, and the Twins rookie Keaschall was the headliner of the pair — a .375 average and .455 OBP in limited action screams "future everyday player," and Philip got in early. Canzone chipped in a homer and three RBI for good measure. Two useful bodies for the price of a waiver claim is a nice week's work, even if it's Bauers wearing the crown.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

We've got a three-way tie this week for worst use of a waiver claim. Dennis kicks things off by handing Schlittler's List a roster spot to Luis García Jr., the former Nationals top prospect who's spent the last couple years reminding everyone why "top prospect" and "finished product" aren't the same thing. The return on investment: a .143 average, zero runs, zero homers, zero RBI, zero steals. Zero of everything except regret.

Jordan's turn. Babe Ruthless rolled the dice on Michael Petersen, a Marlins reliever nobody outside the Petersen household had heard of, and got one inning of work back — a 9.00 ERA, a 2.00 WHIP, and not a single strikeout to show for it. A parking ticket with extra steps.

And Tyler's not walking away clean either. The Bryce Is Right streamed Zebby Matthews, the young Twins righty with real pedigree, and got six innings of 6.00 ERA, a 1.83 WHIP, and a start that didn't come close to earning a quality start despite three strikeouts. The scouting report said "arm talent." The box score said "start looking elsewhere."

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

This week's stream crown goes to Matt. Ep-Skenes Files plucked Sean Burke off the wire and got a genuine ace impression: seven innings, nine strikeouts, a 1.29 ERA, a microscopic 0.57 WHIP, and a quality start locked up before the seventh-inning stretch. That's the kind of line that wins a week by itself, and Matt found it on waivers.

Dennis deserves a mention too. Schlittler's List streamed Eury Pérez, the Marlins phenom who missed real time to Tommy John surgery and is only now rounding back into the form that made him a top pitching prospect in the first place. Six innings, six strikeouts, a 3.00 ERA and a quality start — a real signal that the arm talent everyone raved about pre-surgery is still very much intact.

Jordan rounds it out with Eric Lauer, the well-traveled lefty now doing work in Los Angeles. Six innings, three strikeouts, a 4.50 ERA and a 1.33 WHIP still snuck across the quality-start line. Not a highlight-reel outing, but a serviceable spot start that did its job.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The bench blunders have a running theme this week, and his name is Tom. Professor McGonigle benched Nico Hoerner for the entire week — a steady Cubs table-setter who chipped in a couple steals and a run while stapled to the pine — and also sat Payton Tolle for a day he delivered seven strikeouts in just 3 2/3 innings. Two decisions, two missed opportunities, one very confused front office.

Nate's not escaping this column either. Risky Bettiness benched not one but two starting pitchers for the entire week: Cade Cavalli, the former first-round arm working his way back from Tommy John surgery, quietly stacked six innings, five strikeouts and a quality start on the bench, and Tanner Bibee added six and two-thirds innings with three strikeouts and a quality start of his own — all of it wasted on a roster that apparently forgot both men existed.

Matt can't fully celebrate his Bauers and Burke heroics without answering for benching Taj Bradley on a day the Twins righty went seven innings with six strikeouts and a quality start. And Philip sat Tyler Soderstrom for two days worth of a homer and a couple runs, proving that even a good waiver week doesn't excuse an inactive lineup card.

The week's crown jewel of front office malpractice, though, goes to Jesse. Eephus found a way to bench Shohei Ohtani — as a hitter — for two days, watching him hit a homer from the bench. There is a two-way global icon on that roster, and somehow the lineup card didn't have room for him.

The Insider Report

by "Deep Source" DiNapoli

Eephus front office is doing something. Sources describe a total organizational reset: sabermetrics out, projection models out, and in their place a decision-maker described only as "a suspiciously intelligent baby." I can't independently verify the age of the party allegedly running Jesse's waiver claims, but I can verify the record — 61-122-9, dead last with Risky Bettiness for the rebuilding basement — and something about that .341 win percentage tracks with the theory.

Front offices don't outsource control for no reason. When a team benches conventional wisdom this hard, it's usually because conventional wisdom already failed them once this year. I'm told the pivot came after a string of FAAB misses that had the whole league snickering in the group chat. Whether the new regime actually improves the process or just gives Jesse plausible deniability for the next questionable add, nobody in the league office would confirm on the record.

Worth watching: does Eephus start winning waiver priority battles it has no business winning? Random-chance bidding has a funny way of occasionally out-FAABing the sharks, and if Jesse stumbles into two or three useful streamers off pure chaos theory, expect Chuck to demand a formal investigation. I'm also hearing whispers — nothing confirmed — that at least one other rebuilding club is eyeing the same approach out of pure desperation. Risky Bettiness, sitting right there at 60-124-8, would have nothing to lose.

With the trade deadline still three and a half weeks out, the contenders at the top — Professor McGonigle, Ep-Skenes Files, and Schlittler's List — should be circling both basement teams for salvage deals before this saga sorts itself out. A front office running on baby logic might just say yes to an offer a sharper GM would laugh out of the room. Keep an eye on it. I'm keeping mine.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Risky Bettiness posted the worst ERA in the league this week, 6.88 against a 3.75 average, while throwing just 35 1/3 innings — barely 5 1/3 above the 30-inning floor teams need to clear or forfeit every pitching category. They won anyway. A 7-4-1 decision over Eephus, built entirely off runs, steals, average, OBP, WHIP, K/BB, and saves-plus-holds, categories that had nothing to do with the ERA sitting on their line. A blown ERA doesn't sink a week if the volume everywhere else carries it.

Juiced Balls ran the opposite experiment. Their K/BB of 5.58 led the league, comfortably clear of the 3.31 average, and their ERA of 2.49 sat right behind Ep-Skenes Files' league-best 2.45. WHIP checked in at 0.95, well under the 1.20 league average. That's about as clean a pitching week as this league produces, and it didn't matter. Their offense delivered 17 RBI, the league low, and Jr Jays' 46 R, 46 RBI, .316 AVG, .391 OBP week buried them 5-7-0 regardless of what the arms did.

Babe Ruthless ran into the worst kind of luck this format can produce. Their 5 quality starts tied for the second-best mark in the league, with two other teams also sitting at 5. The only team with more was Schlittler's List, at 7, and Schlittler's List happened to be this week's opponent. Babe Ruthless didn't have a bad week on the mound. They just drew the one matchup where a normally-competitive number was a loser.

One more line worth a second look: Risky Bettiness's K/BB of 4.11 is a genuinely strong strikeout-to-walk mark, well clear of the 3.31 league average. Paired with that 6.88 ERA, the math points to contact allowed as the problem, not walks — hard-hit balls finding grass, not free passes piling up. That's the kind of split that tends to even out over a larger sample. I won't call it luck. I'll call it a small sample doing what small samples do.
