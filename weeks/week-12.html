Week 12 Recap (2026-06-08 — 2026-06-14)

Monday, June 15, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jr Jays went 1-11-0 after the first day against Risky Bettiness, losing eleven categories simultaneously before the week had really started. By Sunday the scoreline read 12-0-0. The comeback was genuine — Jr Jays swept the batting side and flipped pitching categories through midweek — but the perfect score required help from across the field, and Nate provided it.

Risky Bettiness finished at 27.1 innings pitched. The floor is 30. All six pitching categories were forfeited, including ERA (3.95 to 4.67), WHIP (1.39 to 1.42), K/BB (2.90 to 2.88), and SV+H (4 to 2) — four categories that Nate's staff actually won in the numbers. Without the forfeit this lands at 8-4-0, still a Jr Jays win. With it, 12-0-0. Those 2 2/3 innings don't come back.

The most legitimate comeback of the week belongs to Ep-Skenes Files, who trailed Babe Ruthless 0-11-1 after Monday. Zero categories won, eleven lost, one tied. Jordan's team was running up the score while Matt's squad was still looking for an answer. Then Tuesday flipped four categories, Wednesday flipped more, and by the end of the third day Ep-Skenes Files held the lead at 7-4-1 and never looked back. A team winning eleven of twelve categories on Day 1 does not usually lose the matchup. This week they did. Final: 7-4-1.

Professor McGonigle never allowed this matchup to become a contest, leading Eephus 9-2-1 out of the gate and finishing 10-1-1 wire to wire. The batting was close — home runs tied at ten, OBP going to Eephus at .328 to .326, AVG separated by .002 — but the pitching was not. 65 strikeouts to 33, a 1.10 WHIP against 1.33, a K/BB of 5.00 against 2.20. Tom's mound staff took five of six pitching categories and made the whole thing feel inevitable from the start.

Juiced Balls trailed The Bryce Is Right 4-6-2 after the first day, with Tyler's lineup putting up the bigger counting numbers early. Fantasy Guru's pitching staff changed that by Tuesday and held the lead through a 6-4-2 final. A 2.92 ERA and 4.31 K/BB against 4.13 and 2.84 swung the efficiency categories hard. The Bryce Is Right put up 71 strikeouts across 76.1 innings — more on both counts — but ERA, WHIP, and K/BB all went the other way, and SV+H was 3 to 0. The narrowest gap in the matchup was AVG at .004 — .297 to .293 — and Juiced Balls took that one too.

The week's cleanest result: Cease and Desist swept all six batting categories against Kirby's Dream Team and left with a 7-5-0 win. Philip's lineup posted .307 AVG, .402 OBP, 17 home runs, and 45 RBI. Dennis's pitching answered with 3.38 ERA, 1.04 WHIP, five quality starts, and 3.35 K/BB, taking five of six pitching categories. The one that got away was SV+H, where Cease and Desist went 7 to 2. Six batting categories versus five pitching: 7-5-0, and the closest margin anywhere was .040 in AVG.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

James Wood delivered the kind of week that reminds everyone what the hype was about. The Washington outfielder — one of the sport's most promising young hitters — posted nine runs, three home runs, six RBI, three steals, a .440 average, and a .500 OBP for Jr Jays, a complete line that touched every batting category on the board. Wood has the raw power of a corner bat and the wheels of a leadoff man, and on the right week he deploys all of it.

The runners-up made it a genuine competition. Jackson Chourio launched five home runs and drove in ten for Juiced Balls — the Brewers outfielder is turning into one of the more exciting young bats in the game, and a .448 average this week made the power look even cleaner. Byron Buxton put up .458 with four home runs and a .500 OBP for The Bryce Is Right, which is exactly what Buxton does when he's healthy and exactly why his fantasy manager ages in dog years waiting for him to stay that way. The real footnote belongs to Ivan Herrera: a .607 OBP from the catcher spot for Professor McGonigle is a number that demands a double-take, backed by eight runs and a pair of steals.

On the mound, Jacob Latz takes the award on efficiency — 4 1/3 innings, a 0.46 WHIP, six strikeouts, and two saves-plus-holds out of the Texas bullpen for Cease and Desist. The runners-up had legitimate claims. Cristopher Sánchez logged 12 2/3 innings for Professor McGonigle, earned a quality start, and struck out 13 across two outings — the workload argument is real. Cade Smith was just as sharp in a shorter role: a 0.75 WHIP with six punchouts in four innings, also for Professor McGonigle. And Mason Miller racked up eight strikeouts in 3 1/3 innings for Juiced Balls — the kind of stuff that makes hitters look like they showed up to the wrong sport.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

The top tier is intact, but the distance between first and the rest is starting to look less like a gap and more like a canyon.

Matt handles business with a 7-4-1 and holds the three-hole — a clean, competent week from a team that keeps trending the right direction. The gap to first is real; the gap to the teams chasing him from below is real too.

SOLID

Two teams with legitimate season records stuck just outside the top tier. One of them had the week of their lives. The other needs to find out where the week of their life went.

#4 — Jr Jays — 67-69-8 (.493) | W 12-0-0

Gordon's team swept every single category, going 12-0-0, and somehow still sits below .500 on the year — which tells you everything you need to know about where this season has gone before now. #5 — Kirby's Dream Team — 81-58-5 (.580) | L 5-7-0

Dennis owns the best winning percentage in this tier and still dropped a 5-7-0 — a loss he cannot afford against a playoff race that tightens by the week. The season record says this team belongs in Contenders; the recent results say it needs to earn its way back.

MEDIOCRE

Three managers who can beat you on any given week and haven't been able to string those weeks together. The standings gap to the top is getting less theoretical.

#6 — Babe Ruthless — 74-64-6 (.535) | L 4-7-1

The defending champion is posting a 4-7-1 week and drifting toward the middle of the standings — Jordan walked into this league last year and won the whole thing, and watching Babe Ruthless fade into mediocre territory is genuinely uncomfortable to write. There's still time, but the runway is getting shorter.

#7 — Juiced Balls — 61-72-11 (.462) | W 6-4-2

Eleven ties on the season. Eleven. That number is the real story for Fantasy Guru's operation — categories left on the table all year long, and the 6-4-2 win this week only goes so far toward making up for it. Take the win, find those tied categories, and start converting them.

#8 — Cease and Desist — 60-78-6 (.438) | W 7-5-0

Philip's team is deep in the hole at sixty wins against seventy-eight losses, but back-to-back winning weeks means there's still a pulse. The math for a serious playoff run from here is brutal — but at least the body is moving in the right direction.

REBUILDING

There is nothing fun to say here. The data is the commentary.

#9 — Eephus — 47-90-7 (.351) | L 1-10-1

Ninety losses on the year, and the 1-10-1 this week added to the pile — Jesse's squad found one category to win and that was the day. At .351 for the season, the remaining weeks are about pride and draft position, in that order.

#10 — Risky Bettiness — 43-94-7 (.323) | L 0-12-0

Zero categories. Twelve opportunities, zero wins. Nate's team went 0-12-0 this week — the cleanest possible loss — and sits at .323 on the year with no immediate signs of relief. That is the worst single-week result this league has produced all season, and it earns last place without argument.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ryan Weathers wins this one by a comfortable margin. Ep-Skenes Files added him this week and received 4 1/3 innings, a 12.46 ERA, two strikeouts, and no quality start in return. Dropping Christian Scott — a pitcher with legitimate arm talent — to bring in a line like that is the kind of decision that looks worse the longer you stare at it. When the ERA is north of twelve, every inning of work is a liability, and Ep-Skenes Files pitched 4 1/3 of them.

Kirby's Dream Team ran out the Dalton Rushing experiment at catcher, and the LAD backstop delivered a .143 average with zero home runs and zero RBI. Rushing has real tools in his developmental profile, but the swing-and-miss tendencies have followed him, and this week they dominated. A single run scored and a .333 OBP built mostly on walks is not what a manager is hoping for when adding a catcher off the wire.

Risky Bettiness rounds out the shame with Ezequiel Duran — the TEX utility man can play half the positions on the diamond, but the bat logged a .192 average, a .222 OBP, no home runs, and no RBI this week. Three runs scored is the sum total of his category contribution. Versatility has value on a roster, but only when the bat eventually shows up to justify it.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Risky Bettiness logged 27 1/3 innings this week. The floor is 30. The gap was 2 2/3 innings — less than one streaming start — and because that gap existed, all six pitching categories were forfeited. ERA, WHIP, K/BB, and SV+H were all category wins. Risky Bettiness handed four won pitching categories to the opponent because the roster never cleared the minimum threshold.

The bench decisions compound the damage. Tanner Bibee sat on the Risky Bettiness bench on a day he went out and threw seven innings, struck out eight, and earned a quality start. Those innings, in the lineup, represent a meaningful chunk of that 2 2/3-inning gap to the floor. Risky Bettiness also benched Andrew Vaughn on a day he went four hits, a home run, and four RBI. The pitching forfeit and the lineup mismanagement did not happen in isolation — together they turned a winnable week into a disaster.

The rest of the league had its own lapses. Cease and Desist left George Springer in the dugout on a day he scored three runs and hit a home run. Ep-Skenes Files benched Otto Lopez while he scored four times, collected three hits, and stole a base. The Bryce Is Right sat Hunter Goodman through two runs, a homer, and two RBI. Babe Ruthless rested Bryan Reynolds on a day he went two hits, two runs, and a home run. The bench is where fringe players wait — not where contributors collect dust while the innings clock runs short.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The FAAB market barely flinched this week — $89 total spent across the entire league, with six teams still sitting on untouched $200 budgets heading into mid-June. Either this league is loaded with patient operators biding their time, or most managers simply haven't identified the wire talent worth going after. Both explanations have some truth to them. With the trade deadline sitting at August 6th, that unspent FAAB carries real value as a chip for acquiring players who clear waivers in the second half.

Professor McGonigle made the week's lone significant move, winning Braden Montgomery at $87. Montgomery is one of the more intriguing young outfielders on the CWS roster — a former high-end pick finally getting consistent playing time — and the front office is making a long-term bet that the upside justifies nearly half the starting budget. At $53 remaining, Professor McGonigle has narrowed the margin for error considerably. Landen Roupp was the casualty of the move, which was not a trivial drop.

Ep-Skenes Files picked up Ryan Weathers for $2 and collected a 12.46 ERA for the trouble, which earns its own write-up in the Hall of Shame. With $143 banked, Ep-Skenes Files has the runway to recover. At the other end of the spectrum, Kirby's Dream Team enters the week at $0 — the league's only tapped-out roster. The gap between a fully-loaded war chest and an empty one grows more significant every week, and six managers are still in position to act when it actually matters.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Cease and Desist led the league in four batting categories this week — RBI (45), SB (8), AVG (.307), OBP (.402) — tied for the home run lead at 17, and still needed a win in SV+H to secure a 7-5-0 finish. Kirby's Dream Team swept every non-saves pitching category: better ERA (3.38 to 3.75), better WHIP (1.04 to 1.23), better K/BB (3.35 to 2.17), and five quality starts to two. Cease and Desist's K/BB of 2.17 is the worst in the league against a league average of 3.22. Dominant batting carried the week. It won't always.

The Bryce Is Right led the league in runs (47), tied for the home run lead at 17, logged 76.1 innings — the most in the league — and still lost 4-6-2. The ERA on those 76.1 innings was 4.13 with a 1.36 WHIP, and the SV+H total was zero. Juiced Balls had three saves-plus-holds. Losing one category because your bullpen doesn't record a hold or a save all week is avoidable. Losing ERA, WHIP, and K/BB alongside it is the entire pitching section surrendered. The Bryce Is Right outscored the field in runs and still came away with a loss. The pitching roster is where this week fell apart.

Jr Jays swept Risky Bettiness 12-0-0. The mechanism: Risky Bettiness threw 27.1 innings, 2 2/3 short of the 30-inning floor, and forfeited all six pitching categories. Without the forfeit, Risky Bettiness wins ERA (3.95 to 4.67), WHIP (1.39 to 1.42), K/BB (2.90 to 2.88), and SV+H (4 to 2) — four of six pitching categories. Jr Jays would have won K (49 to 29) and quality starts (3 to 1), making a plausible final score 8-4-0. The sweep reflects the forfeit, not the performance. Risky Bettiness was already losing this matchup decisively; missing the innings floor by 2 2/3 converted a bad week into an unusual line in the standings.
