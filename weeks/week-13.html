Week 13 Recap (2026-06-15 — 2026-06-21)

Tuesday, June 23, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Down 1-6-5 after Monday, trailing 4-7-1 through Saturday, Professor McGonigle had no business winning this matchup. Tom's squad engineered a Sunday surge that doesn't show up in any single stat swing but lands loud in the final score: 6-5-1, a win Tyler had in his back pocket until the last day. The Bryce Is Right held the pitching edge all week — better K, ERA, WHIP, and K/BB — but the batting categories broke Tom's way, and .038 in AVG was the margin that finished it.

Philip also earned his win the hard way. Cease and Desist trailed Ep-Skenes Files by three categories on opening day, took the lead Thursday, surrendered it again Saturday when Matt's team went up 8-4-0, then closed it out Sunday — three lead changes in six days. Philip's ERA came in at 1.80 on the week; Matt's was 3.27. Nine saves-plus-holds from Cease and Desist pushed the pitching edge past the tipping point. RBI finished knotted at 35, AVG came down to .002 apart. Every win in that box score was earned.

Fantasy Guru had an announcement to make this week. Juiced Balls posted a 10-2-0 over Risky Bettiness — 40 runs, a .302 average, a 1.04 WHIP, a 3.47 K/BB — and Nate's team had no answer. The only categories Nate salvaged were HR (13 to 8) and RBI (35 to 34). The closest single category all week was OBP at eight points. When the tightest gap in a matchup is eight OBP points and it still goes against you, the week was gone before it started.

Kirby's Dream Team trailed Gordon's Jr Jays for most of the week — ahead on zero days heading into Sunday. Then Dennis's pitching staff turned in a 2.31 ERA, 0.86 WHIP, and a 10.13 K/BB for the week, swept all six pitching categories, and flipped a 5-6-1 into a 7-5-0 win on the final day. Gordon's team hit well — 30 R, 38 RBI, .275 AVG — and took five of six batting categories, including a razor-thin OBP edge (.338 to .335), but a 6.34 ERA and 1.66 WHIP on the mound made the pitching side untenable. Dennis needed Sunday and got it.

Jordan ran wire-to-wire and Babe Ruthless made it look workmanlike: 8-4-0 over Jesse's Eephus, .300 team average, 41 RBI, nine stolen bases against zero from Jesse's side. Jesse hit 15 HR to Jordan's 9 and posted the better ERA and WHIP — 5.29 to 6.41, 1.42 to 1.53 — but two pitching categories weren't enough to swing the total. The K/BB column, 2.46 to 2.42 in Jordan's favor, was the closest call in a matchup that was settled early and never recovered its suspense.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Pete Crow-Armstrong turned Ep-Skenes Files into the envy of the league this week, and I will not hear any argument to the contrary. The Cubs outfielder went .550/.600 with four home runs, seven RBI, five runs, and a pair of stolen bases — a complete offensive demolition from a player who already had the reputation for his glove and his speed. Now he's adding the bat to that toolkit in a big way, and Matt is sitting on something dangerous. Crow-Armstrong wins Batter of the Week going away.

The runners-up had no shot, but they went down swinging. Travis Bazzana hit .500/.591 with three home runs and seven RBI for Eephus — the young Cleveland second baseman is building a case that Jesse's roster construction deserves more credit. Kyle Schwarber posted nine runs, five home runs, and ten RBI for Risky Bettiness, which is vintage Schwarber: all power, no apologies. George Springer went .391/.444 with three stolen bases for Cease and Desist, and three bags from Springer is not what anyone penciled in, but that is the kind of veteran wildcard week that makes Philip insufferable in the group chat.

Louis Varland wins Pitcher of the Week on efficiency. Five and a third innings, nine strikeouts, a 0.56 WHIP, and four saves-plus-holds from the Toronto reliever — he did his damage in a limited sample and the ratios are the proof. Drew Rasmussen turned in two quality starts across 13 innings with a 2.08 ERA for Juiced Balls, a 6:1 strikeout-to-walk ratio making it even cleaner. Nolan McLean went seven innings with nine strikeouts and exactly one walk for Jr Jays — a K/BB of 9.00 is command, pure and simple. And Kyle Bradish, back for Kirby's Dream Team after Tommy John surgery, went 11 and two-thirds innings, punched out 16, and posted a 0.77 ERA — the return has been worth the wait.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

CONTENDERS

The top three teams have separated from the rest of this league, and the gap between third place and fourth is more than a few wins — it's a different conversation entirely.

#1 Professor McGonigle — 97-51-8 (.647) — W, 6-5-1 Tom's team is the standard against which every other roster in this league is being measured, and 97 wins in the H2H format with weeks still to play makes that case without any help from this column. The 6-5-1 this week was a grind, not a showcase, but first place doesn't negotiate on style points.

#2 Kirby's Dream Team — 88-63-5 (.580) — W, 7-5-0 Dennis went 7-5-0 and takes the No. 2 spot in these rankings, though the nine-game gap to Tom is the only number that matters for anyone thinking about the top of the bracket. This team is built to win in October — getting there with leverage is the remaining assignment.

#3 The Bryce Is Right — 88-60-8 (.590) — L, 5-6-1 Here is the scheduling irony baked into H2H formats: Tyler actually carries a higher season win percentage than the team ranked directly above him and still sits third after a 5-6-1 loss this week. The Bryce Is Right cannot afford many more stumbles heading into the stretch, no matter what the cumulative numbers say.

SOLID

Two teams chasing the top tier without quite having the goods to break in — yet.

#4 Babe Ruthless — 82-68-6 (.545) — W, 8-4-0 An 8-4-0 week from the reigning champion is a reminder that Jordan knows what winning looks like in this league because he did it twelve months ago. Babe Ruthless is the most dangerous team outside the Contenders tier, and anyone sleeping on him this late in the season has a short memory.

#5 Juiced Balls — 71-74-11 (.490) — W, 10-2-0 Fantasy Guru went 10-2-0 this week. The season record is a real problem; that weekly output is a real warning shot. If Juiced Balls can string even two weeks at that level, the back half of this season gets uncomfortable for teams ranked above him.

MEDIOCRE

Three managers hovering near the playoff cutline, none of them particularly comfortable with where things stand.

#6 Ep-Skenes Files — 82-67-7 (.548) — L, 4-7-1 Matt carries a .548 season win percentage — better than both teams ranked ahead of him in these tiers — yet here he sits after a 4-7-1 loss dropped him into Mediocre territory. The season résumé argues for more; a week like that is why he doesn't have it.

#7 Cease and Desist — 67-82-7 (.452) — W, 7-4-1 Philip won 7-4-1 this week, which is the most encouraging thing out of the Cease and Desist operation in some time. The 67-82-7 record is what it is, but wins still count and you have to start somewhere.

#8 Jr Jays — 72-76-8 (.487) — L, 5-7-0 Gordon dropped 5-7-0 and sits just under .500 with the postseason window narrowing by the week. The Jr Jays have been in this range all season — close enough to matter, not close enough to move anyone.

REBUILDING

The season has been educational. The lesson has been expensive.

#9 Eephus — 51-98-7 (.349) — L, 4-8-0 Jesse's debut season has produced a 51-98-7 record, and a 4-8-0 loss this week does not improve the grade. This league is a hard teacher in year one, and Eephus is getting a thorough education in every chapter.

#10 Risky Bettiness — 45-104-7 (.311) — L, 2-10-0 Two wins in twelve categories this week. Nate's Risky Bettiness is sitting at 45-104-7 with the end of the season in sight, and that record is going to be the first thing brought up at every draft discussion between now and next spring.

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Dennis was first to the Jac Caglianone window and Kirby's Dream Team has the week to show for it. The former first-round Royals pick delivered two home runs, three runs, three RBI, and a .444 OBP — that on-base number is the headline, because it means Caglianone was grinding at-bats, working counts, and producing across multiple stat columns that matter in category leagues.

Philip's Cease and Desist earns runner-up recognition with the Wilyer Abreu add. Three runs, a home run, two RBI, a stolen base, and a .385 OBP — four categories touched from a waiver claim is a strong return. Fantasy Guru's Willi Castro add for Juiced Balls contributed three RBI and a .250 average, which is useful filler without being the kind of move that generates column space.

Caglianone takes the top spot going away. Power bats with that pedigree do not linger on the wire, and Dennis found him first.

Castro delivered a floor and a ceiling in one week, which is a unique way to land in two categories simultaneously.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Tyler's The Bryce Is Right went to the wire twice and Dustin May was the better of the two returns. The Cardinals right-hander gave Tyler 11 innings, 11 strikeouts, a 0.82 WHIP, and a 5.50 K/BB ratio, earning a quality start in the process. The ERA came in at 4.91, which keeps this from being a complete streaming line, but a pitcher who strikes out a batter per inning while holding the WHIP under 1.00 is worth the roster spot. May has been working his way back from Tommy John, and this week's command metrics suggest the process is moving in the right direction.

Will Warren added the secondary contribution — 5 2/3 innings, eight strikeouts, a 3.18 ERA, and a 4.00 K/BB ratio. Warren did not earn a quality start, but the strikeout production and the command in under six innings are both streaming-worthy results. The Bryce Is Right assembled two useful outings off the wire and got real pitching value in return.

Matt's Ep-Skenes Files made the closest challenge with the Eric Lauer stream: 12 innings, a 2.25 ERA, a 1.00 WHIP, and a quality start. The volume is the story with Lauer — 12 innings of 2.25 ERA pitching is a genuine contribution to any staff. The 1.00 K/BB ratio keeps the strikeout ceiling low, which is where May edges him. Matt made a smart play; Tyler's May line was just the better streaming result.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Stephen Kolek was on Tyler's The Bryce Is Right roster until Tyler cut him loose. Gordon's Jr Jays were immediately there to scoop him off the wire, which means Tyler released an asset that went straight into a competitor's lineup. Dropping a pitcher who turns into someone else's streaming resource is a quiet mistake, and Tyler has had enough busy weeks that one more transaction going the wrong direction starts to add up.

The dead weight file runs long this week. Drake Baldwin has hit .083 over 24 at-bats across the past three weeks for Kirby's Dream Team — his OBP matches that average exactly, meaning zero walks, every plate appearance producing an out or the occasional lonely single. Dennis won the best pickup of the week with Caglianone, which makes the Baldwin situation all the more puzzling. The move that upgrades one roster spot while ignoring another is only half the job.

The defending champion takes two entries. Jordan's Babe Ruthless is carrying Willy Adames at .121 over 33 at-bats and Jarren Duran at .138 over 65 at-bats. The Duran number is the one that demands attention: 65 at-bats is a full reckoning, and a .153 OBP means he is not reaching base by any route. Duran had a genuine breakout season that made him a roster cornerstone entering 2026, and the current version has not shown up. Jordan won this league in 2025. The roster he is running right now does not look like a repeat effort.

The remaining entries: Carter Jensen at .133 over 30 at-bats for Ep-Skenes Files, Spencer Steer at .125 over 48 at-bats for the Jr Jays, and Alejandro Kirk at .143 with a .161 OBP for Eephus. Kirk's OBP is the number worth noting — a catcher with his contact history posting an on-base mark just 18 points above his average means he is not drawing walks, either. The wire has options. These decisions are overdue.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Kirby's Dream Team issued 8 walks and struck out 81 batters, for a K/BB of 10.13. The league average was 3.58; the next-best figure this week was 3.50. The staff also led the league in WHIP (0.86), quality starts (7), and strikeouts while posting a 2.31 ERA — four pitching category leaders in one week. The final result was 7-5-0, which is correct, because five of six batting categories went to Jr Jays: 23 RBI tied for the league low, .255 AVG and .335 OBP didn't help. The pitching did everything it could.

Babe Ruthless went 8-4-0 with a 6.41 ERA — the worst in the league, 2.35 runs above the league mean. Jordan's lineup hit .300, drove in 41, stole 9 bases, and scored 33 runs, which won five batting categories and pulled three pitching wins along with it. The pitching wins are worth noting: strikeouts (32 to 29), quality starts (5 to 1), K/BB (2.46 to 2.42 — a margin of four hundredths). All three came against an Eephus rotation that threw just 32 1/3 innings, barely clearing the 30-inning floor. The 6.41 ERA and the 8-4-0 record coexist for now.

Ep-Skenes Files posted 6 saves-plus-holds — the second-highest total in the league. Every other team finished with fewer, except one: Cease and Desist, who produced 9, and who happened to be Matt's matchup opponent. Against any other draw this week, that SV+H figure wins the category outright. Instead it went to Cease and Desist, and Matt came out on the wrong side of a 4-7-1 final.
