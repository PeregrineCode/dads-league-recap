Monday, April 6, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Five days. Eephus led Juiced Balls for five consecutive days, holding a 5-6 edge going into Saturday with a matchup that looked sealed. Then Sunday happened, and the Fantasy Guru's pitching staff filed a formal objection. Juiced Balls' K/BB ratio finished at 6.22 for the week — that's not just winning a category, that's what Randy Johnson did to opposing lineups in 2001. By the time Sunday's dust settled, Juiced Balls had flipped the entire board and walked away with an 8-3 blowout that was a 5-6 loss the night before. Eephus hit .244, had 4 quality starts, and accumulated 28 RBI, which isn't nothing — but in a 12-category league, posting zero stolen bases is a white flag. Not one. Hand Juiced Balls a free category and see what happens.

Over in the most technically embarrassing loss of Week 2, Risky Bettiness closed out the matchup with 27 innings pitched. The floor is 30. Three innings short — that's one mid-rotation spot start, and Risky Bettiness didn't have it. All six pitching categories were forfeited on the spot. The absurdity is this: Risky Bettiness would have lost K, ERA, WHIP, and QS anyway. But their K/BB of 3.22 would have beaten Cease and Desist's 2.48, and their 6 saves-plus-holds would have crushed C&D's 1. Two categories given away for free. Without the forfeit, Risky Bettiness wins this matchup 6-5. Instead, they lose 7-4. Three innings short of the minimum. Three innings short of a win.

Professor McGonigle and Babe Ruthless staged the best actual competition of the week. Babe Ruthless looked like a winner — 10 HR, 41 runs, 34 RBI, a power week that would make any manager feel comfortable — and held a 6-4 advantage deep into Saturday. McGonigle answered in the only way left: winning the ratio categories, stealing the stolen base battle 9-to-5, and then on the final day, running down Babe Ruthless to complete a 7-5 comeback. The decisive margin? OBP, .381 to .363 — eighteen points. That's a masterclass in playing to your strengths when your opponent is trying to bury you with the long ball.

All Betts Are Off delivered a wire-to-wire 8-3 dismantling of Jr Jays, and the pitching line is the only paragraph you need: 1.27 ERA and a 0.86 WHIP. I'll give Jr Jays credit for showing up — they took SB, AVG, and OBP — but matching a pitching staff posting those numbers is like asking someone to outscore the '95 Braves rotation. All Betts Are Off's offense was a .188 batting average and a .272 OBP, which is not good, and it simply did not matter. Not even a little.

Misio Soup ran Ep-Skenes Files out of the building 7-4, powered by an offensive nuclear event: 16 home runs and 55 RBI in a single week. Fifty-five RBI. Ep-Skenes Files posted a 3.02 ERA and 1.15 WHIP, won the stolen base battle 9-to-2, and still couldn't find the exit fast enough. You don't lose a 7-4 matchup because your pitching failed — you lose it because the other team's lineup decided to cosplay as the 2019 Twins. Quality starts were knotted at 3 apiece, the only number in this matchup that didn't embarrass somebody.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

POWER RANKINGS — WEEK 2

CONTENDERS

Three teams have separated themselves from the field, and I will not hear any arguments about it.

#1 — All Betts Are Off (15-8-1) — The best record in this league, an 8-3 demolition job this week, and it wasn't an accident. This is what a well-managed team looks like, and everyone else is playing for second place until further notice.

#2 — Misio Soup (14-9-1) — A 7-4 win keeps them right on All Betts' heels, and I'm not ready to write them off the top spot yet. Four games back in the loss column is manageable. Don't blink.

#3 — Juiced Balls (11-11-2) — Here's the controversial pick and I'm standing by it. You don't go 8-3 in a single week without doing something right, and that kind of category dominance earns you a seat at the contenders table. The overall record is mediocre, sure — I see you — but the floor just moved.

SOLID

Winning weeks, solid records, legitimate threats. Just not elite. Yet.

#4 — Cease and Desist (12-11-1) — Took a 7-4 win and still somehow feels like they're underachieving. The record says .521 and the vibes say the same. More of a "don't sleep on them in a playoff matchup" team than a "they're going to run the table" team.

#5 — Professor McGonigle (12-10-2) — Squeaked out a 7-5 win, which means the Commissioner's team is alive and feisty and absolutely will be insufferable about it. The .542 winning percentage is real. The two-game cushion in wins is real. The skepticism is also real.

MEDIOCRE

We're not panicking. But we're watching.

#6 — Babe Ruthless (13-10-1) — Yes, I know the record is 13-10-1. Yes, I know that's better than the two teams above them. I don't care. A 5-7 loss is a 5-7 loss, and momentum matters in this format. They're sliding, and I'm docking them accordingly. Prove me wrong.

#7 — Ep-Skenes Files (9-12-3) — Three ties on the season tells me this team cannot close. That's a personality flaw in fantasy baseball. The 4-7 loss doesn't help the case.

#8 — Eephus (10-12-2) — An 3-8 week is not a learning experience. It's a problem. The first-year manager is getting a real education right now, and the tuition is steep.

REBUILDING

I'm not sugarcoating it. These teams are in trouble.

#9 — Risky Bettiness (9-14-1) — Nine wins. Fourteen losses. One tie that didn't help anyone. A 4-7 week to keep the bad times rolling. This is what a roster in distress looks like, and waiver wire heroics are the only prescription.

#10 — Jr Jays (7-15-2) — Seven wins. In two weeks of play. I've seen expansion franchises with better early returns. The 3-8 week was not a fluke — it was a confirmation. Someone make a move. Do something. Anything.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jordan Walker hit .300/.382 with 2 home runs, 8 RBI, and 7 runs scored, and Misio Soup was holding the claim slip. Walker has been one of the game's great tantalizing figures — a Cardinals outfielder with elite athleticism and raw power that scouts were writing love letters about before he ever faced a big-league starter. The contact consistency never arrived on schedule, and early-career struggles made him easy to write off. This week, all of it clicked.

Seven runs. Eight RBI. An OBP north of .380 that tells you Walker wasn't just feasting on hanging breaking balls — he was working counts, making pitchers work, and looking like the player the Cardinals drafted him to be. That is a legitimate run-producer's stat line off the waiver wire, and Misio Soup, "The Prospector," found the vein. The rest of the league was panning the same stream and missed it.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The Hall of Shame is supposed to be simple. You find the bad decisions, I explain why they were bad, everyone nods, we go home. This week the irony is embedded in the execution, not the production — which, in some ways, makes it worse.

Professor McGonigle picked up Andrew Painter. That is the correct call. Painter is a 22-year-old Philadelphia starter returning from Tommy John surgery, operating on a strict innings limit while the Phillies manage him like an irreplaceable artifact — carefully, reverently, and with a plan. He pitched this week. He delivered a 1.69 ERA, 8 strikeouts, and a K/BB of 8. He did all of this from Professor McGonigle's bench. That is the entire story. You cannot add Andrew Painter to your roster and then leave Andrew Painter in the dugout. The Phillies are rationing his innings. McGonigle rationed them further.

Misio Soup's entry is more philosophical. Erik Sabrowski is a Guardians reliever, not a known closer, not a frontline arm, not anyone's top priority on the wire. He posted a 0.00 ERA, a 0.69 WHIP, and 4 saves and holds in 4.1 innings. The outcome is fine. The process — grabbing a random bullpen arm and hoping he saves a matchup — is a coin flip dressed up as roster management. It came up heads. Misio Soup should not be taking victory laps until it comes up tails a few times first.

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

The hardware goes to Erik Sabrowski, middle reliever for the Cleveland Guardians, and your Stream of the Week for a performance nobody saw coming.

Four saves and holds. Zero earned runs in 4.1 innings. A 0.69 WHIP with 7 strikeouts from a streaming spot. In head-to-head categories where SV+H is its own line on the scoreboard, a week like that does not just help — it flips matchups, and Misio Soup was the beneficiary. Andrew Painter gets the honorable mention — an 8-K start with a K/BB of 8 from a young Philadelphia arm coming off Tommy John is exactly what you want when you stream a starting pitcher, and if the Phillies ever take the training wheels off him he will be an elite target all season. But Painter went 5.1 innings, registered zero quality starts, and his own manager sat him during the week. Sabrowski was deployed. Sabrowski delivered. That's the category.

The lesson here is narrow: this was a great week from a reliever who earned the recognition. Whether Sabrowski is a keeper is a question for next week's column.

Trade Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Two trades crossed the wire this week, and one of them will be referenced in October when somebody is fighting for a playoff spot.

Let's go straight to the headline. All Betts Are Off acquired Bryce Harper and Kyle Harrison. Ep-Skenes Files received Rafael Devers. Take a moment. Devers is a legitimate star — San Francisco's franchise first baseman who has been producing at a high level for the better part of a decade, a left-handed power bat you build an offense around. He is one player. Harper is Bryce Harper: a former MVP, one of the most dangerous hitters in the National League when he is locked in, the face of a Philadelphia lineup that keeps winning despite itself. And Ep-Skenes Files gave him away, then threw in Kyle Harrison for good measure. All Betts Are Off got two players — including one of the ten best first basemen in fantasy baseball — and paid with one. That is not a trade. That is a masterclass in negotiating leverage. Call Tyler from All Betts Are Off "The Closer" this week, because he shut that deal before the other side knew what was happening.

The McGonigle-Misio Soup exchange is the tighter call. Misio Soup consolidated two roster spots into one, trading Nico Hoerner and Chandler Simpson for Josh Naylor. Naylor is a real power bat — Seattle's first baseman with pop, a legitimate RBI producer, exactly the thump you want in the middle of the order. And the open roster spot isn't nothing — Misio Soup can fill it off the wire with a contributor immediately. Hoerner is a .280-average stabilizer at second base with one of the cleanest contact profiles in the Cubs lineup, and Simpson brings stolen base upside in a league where SB is won and lost on the margins. Both sides can defend this one. Slight edge to McGonigle for landing two contributors who fill distinct category needs.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

We begin with Risky Bettiness, because this one requires no setup. Thirty innings pitched is the minimum. It is posted publicly, enforced consistently, and not a surprise to anyone in this league. Risky Bettiness pitched 27 innings and forfeited all six pitching categories. Their K, ERA, WHIP, and QS were losses regardless. But their K/BB of 3.22 would have beaten Cease and Desist's 2.48, and their 6 saves-plus-holds would have demolished C&D's 1. Two categories given away. Two categories that would have flipped this matchup from a loss to a win. They had the pitching. They didn't have the innings. Three more frames from any available arm in the free agent pool — a spot starter, a middling reliever, a guy with a pulse and a uniform — and Risky Bettiness walks away with a category. Instead they walked away with nothing. That is an organizational failure, and I will not be softening the language.

Misio Soup's drop column this week reads like a front office blooper reel. Joey Wiemer, released from the Misio Soup roster, responded by hitting .476 with 2 home runs, 4 RBI, and 7 runs scored. Wiemer is a toolsy Nationals outfielder with genuine raw power and a swing-and-miss profile that has made him easy to move on from — scouts have been arguing about his floor for years. He chose this specific week to settle the debate. Royce Lewis was shown the door at the same time and replied with 6 runs, 2 home runs, and 5 RBI. Lewis is one awkward slide away from the IL at any given moment and the whole league knows it, but when he plays he is one of the most explosive hitters in the American League. Misio Soup released two of them in the same week and still somehow won the Best Pickup award. It is genuinely impressive how much roster activity one manager fit into seven days.

Cease and Desist held Will Warren for his first start — 4.1 solid innings — then dropped him before his second. Warren went out and delivered an even better encore: the kind of outing that makes rotation spots permanent. Combined line for the week: 10 innings, 2.70 ERA, 9 strikeouts. Cease and Desist got half and gave away the better half. And Professor McGonigle parted ways with Dominic Canzone, who answered with 2 home runs, 5 runs, and a .333 OBP. To close the loop on the Professor this week: he dropped a hitter who produced, picked up a pitcher and sat him while he produced. There are many ways to run a fantasy roster. The Professor appears to be inventing a third.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Risky Bettiness logged 27 innings this week — three short of the mandatory 30-inning floor. All pitching categories forfeited. K, ERA, WHIP, and QS were going to be losses anyway. K/BB was not: 3.22 against Cease and Desist's 2.48. SV+H was not: 6 against 1. Two categories they were winning, handed back without a fight. Run the counterfactual: without the forfeit, Risky Bettiness wins this matchup 6-5. With it, they lose 7-4. The entire result reversed by three innings.

Misio Soup posted 16 home runs and 55 RBI. League averages were 7.6 and 29.1. Both figures are roughly double the mean — not a hot week so much as a different distribution entirely. Misio also led the league in hits, runs, AVG, and OBP, making for one of the more complete single-week offensive performances the data has seen. Their K/BB of 2.07 was dead last, which is a problem they can either solve or outscore. Based on 55 RBI, they appear to be outsourcing it.

All Betts Are Off batted .188 — 57 points below the league average — with a .272 OBP, worst mark in the league by 34 points. They went 8-4-1. The explanation is a 1.27 ERA against a league average of 3.54, a 0.86 WHIP, and 5 quality starts. They lost every batting ratio category and won decisively anyway. In a 12-category format, a large enough advantage across six categories covers six losses cleanly. All Betts found the floor on offense and apparently the ceiling on pitching, and the arithmetic did the rest.

Juiced Balls produced 18 RBI — league worst — and batted .197. Their K/BB was 6.22, against a league average of 3.10. Twice the mean. They went 8-4-1. Two teams this week posting near-identical records while barely hitting: one carried by ERA and WHIP, one by a strikeout-to-walk ratio that is genuinely unusual. The category format does not care how you accumulate your wins. It just counts them.
