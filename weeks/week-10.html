Week 10 Recap (2026-05-25 — 2026-05-31)

Monday, June 1, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ten categories. That was the deficit Babe Ruthless stared at heading into the final day against Juiced Balls — nine to nothing on Monday, and still ten to nothing through Friday as Juiced Balls piled up homers, steals, and ratios all week long. Then Sunday rewrote the week. Babe Ruthless swept enough categories to nip the final result 6-5-1, with the AVG column landing at an exact .304 deadlock on both sides — the most fitting coda to a week where Juiced Balls did nearly everything right and still went home empty-handed.

Kirby's Dream Team delivered the week's most lopsided result, a 9-3-0 dismantling of Risky Bettiness. Forty-one runs, 12 home runs, a 3.17 ERA, a 1.10 WHIP — Risky Bettiness answered with a .341 OBP, a 4.33 K/BB, and 2 saves-plus-holds, a week worth being proud of in most contexts. The 23-hit gap in H/AB told the story early. Three wins for the losing side is a respectable tally; it just happens to mean nine for Kirby's Dream Team.

Jr Jays controlled the Eephus matchup from opening day through the final buzzer, an 8-4-0 wire-to-wire result that peaked at 10-1-1 on Thursday. Eephus took the home runs (13 to 12), edged the RBI (36 to 35), and grabbed the K/BB and saves-plus-holds columns. Still lost by four categories. A 3.50 ERA against 3.71, a 1.22 WHIP against 1.43 — Jr Jays took the pitching categories that mattered and held firm all week.

The Bryce Is Right outlasted Cease and Desist 6-5-1 in a matchup that took until Thursday to swing decisively. Cease and Desist led the first three days and peaked at 7-5-0 on Wednesday before The Bryce Is Right flipped control and never gave it back. By Saturday the lead had stretched to 9-3-0, then Cease and Desist closed the gap on the final day to reach the 6-5-1 final. OBP came down to .332 to .339 — the closest call in the matchup — but 13 home runs and 38 RBI to 7 and 25 made the batting side a foregone conclusion, and a 3.20 ERA with a 1.01 WHIP sealed it.

Professor McGonigle beat Ep-Skenes Files 7-5-0 on the strength of a pitching staff that had no business losing: a 2.04 ERA, a 0.98 WHIP, and a 4.75 K/BB. Ep-Skenes Files outgunned the offense — 41 runs to 31, 8 home runs to 5, 35 RBI to 20 — and piled up 81 strikeouts and 10 saves-plus-holds. None of it overcame a 5.09 ERA and 1.25 WHIP. When the pitching falls apart at that level, winning the batting race on points does not save you.

Players of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ronald Acuña Jr. put up the kind of week that makes every manager in the league sick to their stomach — unless they're Juiced Balls. Five runs, five home runs, ten RBI, five stolen bases, .333 average, .517 OBP. A perfect storm across every batting category that matters. Acuña has spent two years reminding the baseball world what a fully healthy version of him looks like, and this was that reminder written in capital letters. When he's locked in, there's no archetype in fantasy baseball that compares — elite speed, elite power, elite on-base presence, all in one lineup slot.

The runner-up field was no slouch. James Wood hit .435 with a .536 OBP for Jr Jays — a 22-year-old in Washington already forcing scouts to run out of words. Ben Rice (.462, 11 RBI) gave Babe Ruthless a monster contribution out of what was supposed to be a part-time Yankees role. And Oneil Cruz delivered a well-rounded multi-category week for Kirby's Dream Team, the kind of raw talent display that makes Pittsburgh fans simultaneously thrilled and nervous every single time.

Braxton Ashcraft claimed Pitcher of the Week for Cease and Desist, and the margin wasn't debatable. Twelve and a third innings, 16 strikeouts, 2.19 ERA, 1.05 WHIP, two quality starts, and a 16-to-1 strikeout-to-walk ratio. The Pittsburgh righty has been developing quietly in the background all season, and this was the week "developing" stopped being a qualifier. Trevor Megill (0.50 WHIP, three saves-plus-holds) and Justin Wrobleski (7 IP, 0.14 WHIP) both gave The Bryce Is Right elite contributions — but Ashcraft's two-start, 16-strikeout performance operated in a different tier entirely.

Power Rankings

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Contenders. Three teams have separated themselves from the field, and the gap at the top is starting to look permanent.

1. Professor McGonigle — A 75-win pace with a .650 winning percentage is the kind of dominance that doesn't happen by accident, and the Professor keeps collecting wins like he's grading on a curve nobody else can pass. Until someone strings together three or four straight wins against him, this is his league to lose.

2. Kirby's Dream Team — A 9-3-0 beatdown this week is the kind of statement win that reshuffles the conversation, and Dennis's squad is close enough to the top spot that McGonigle should be looking over his shoulder. Four wins back of the top spot with plenty of season left — the dream is still very much alive.

3. The Bryce Is Right — Three wins in the Contenders tier, and Tyler's first-year squad has no business being this good — and yet here we are. A 6-5-1 grind to stay in the top three shows the kind of resilience that separates pretenders from contenders.

Solid. Two teams sitting right at the playoff bubble, close enough to smell the postseason but not close enough to feel comfortable.

4. Babe Ruthless — A 6-5-1 win keeps Jordan in the thick of things, and the defending champion knows how to win when it matters. At .554, Ruthless has the résumé to be taken seriously, even if the team's ceiling is still unclear.

5. Ep-Skenes Files — A 5-7-0 loss when you're sitting at .554 is the kind of week that doesn't knock you out but absolutely cannot become a habit. Matt's team has the talent to be in the Contenders conversation — the question is whether they can stop handing weeks away.

Mediocre. Three teams stuck in the mud, none of them bad enough to quit on, none of them good enough to trust.

6. Jr Jays — An 8-4-0 thumping moves Gordon's squad to the healthiest position in this tier, and suddenly the playoff picture doesn't look quite so grim. A .467 mark still means more losses than wins, but an 8-4-0 week earns some optimism.

7. Juiced Balls — A 5-6-1 loss from Fantasy Guru, and the story of this team all season has been staying just close enough to make you think they'll turn it around. At .429, the window is closing faster than they probably realize.

8. Cease and Desist — Philip's squad also drops 5-6-1, and the problem with being .408 in June is that the math to make the playoffs starts requiring help from other teams. Cease and Desist needs a run, and they needed it two weeks ago.

Rebuilding. Two teams that are either playing out the string or cooking up something nobody's noticed yet. The Hammer believes the former.

9. Eephus — A 4-8-0 week for Jesse's first-year squad, and .375 is a number that demands serious roster introspection. The eephus pitch works because nobody expects it — somebody explain what the plan is here, because it's not showing up in the standings.

10. Risky Bettiness — A 3-9-0 week drops Nate to .346, and at this point the Power Rankings have become a weekly exercise in documenting the decline. The Hammer has seen teams climb out of deeper holes, but usually they stop losing first.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Jeremy Peña, .348 average, 3 home runs, 6 runs, off the waiver wire. The Bryce Is Right identified the window and got there first. Peña has been one of fantasy's more inconsistent shortstops since his 2022 World Series MVP run, and this week he looked like the best version of himself — every category lit up, from the counting stats to the slash line.

The runners-up deserve credit. Babe Ruthless grabbed Willy Adames and watched him post a .320 average with 2 home runs and 6 RBI — a genuine haul for a pickup. The Bryce Is Right also snagged Christian Walker, though Walker's .120/.214 slash line makes that particular decision a lot harder to celebrate. The Peña pickup carries this week's honors; the Walker experiment is a separate conversation.

Hall of Shame

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Professor McGonigle went to the Chicago White Sox waiver wire this week, which is where waiver pickups go to become cautionary tales. Sam Antonacci is a 2B/3B/OF hybrid on the most depleted roster in baseball, and he delivered zero runs, zero home runs, zero RBI, and a .167 average. The lone stolen base may be the loneliest silver lining in this league's history.

Eephus picked up Ian Happ and received a .222 average with .222 OBP — meaning Happ didn't draw a single walk all week. Happ's entire waiver-wire argument has always rested on his ability to generate on-base value. When the OBP goes missing, the whole case collapses with it.

The headliner belongs to Ep-Skenes Files, who streamed Coleman Crow out of Milwaukee and got 4 innings, 1 strikeout, a 4.50 ERA, a 1.50 WHIP, and a K/BB of 0.50 in return. Fewer punchouts than walks, barely enough innings to count, and not a quality start in sight. This one goes in the ledger under "did not check the advanced metrics before hitting add."

Stream of the Week

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Ben Brown went 7 innings, posted a 1.29 ERA and a 0.57 WHIP, struck out 6, and put up a K/BB of 6. The Bryce Is Right went to the Cubs rotation and pulled out the week's best streaming line. Brown has spent significant stretches of his career fighting through arm trouble, and when he's healthy and locked in like this, the results are a reminder of why he was a top prospect coming up through Chicago's system.

Babe Ruthless followed with Keider Montero out of Detroit — 6 scoreless innings, a 0.33 WHIP, a quality start. Montero is a young arm building a reputation in the American League, and this week he backed up the buzz. Cease and Desist got Aaron Nola for 6 solid innings, a 3.00 ERA, and a 0.50 WHIP — which is, more or less, exactly what streaming Aaron Nola looks like. Consistent, workmanlike, bankable.

Front Office Failures

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

Risky Bettiness sat Gerrit Cole for the entire week. Cole threw 6 2/3 innings, struck out 10 batters, and earned a quality start — from the bench. Cole has been one of the most reliable fantasy aces in the game for years. Leaving him on the pine for seven straight days means the rest of the league got a free pass on the strikeouts and the ratios he would have contributed.

That same front office also benched Cade Cavalli for the week — 6 innings, 7 strikeouts, a quality start. Two pitchers, two quality starts, 17 combined strikeouts, none of it deployed. This is a front office that apparently has pitchers and prefers not to use them.

Kirby's Dream Team just traded for Mookie Betts and then sat him for 2 days while he produced 2 home runs and 6 RBI. Gunnar Henderson spent one day on the bench and contributed 2 home runs and 3 RBI during it. The whole point of trading for elite players is to put them in the lineup.

The Bryce Is Right left Hunter Goodman on the bench for four days of play — 2 home runs, 4 RBI left uncollected. Eephus sat Jo Adell through 2 home runs and 4 RBI, and also benched Ernie Clement for 2 active days. Every team gets into lineup jams, but this week the league collectively left a significant amount of production sitting in street clothes.

Transaction Desk

by Chuck "The Hammer" Morrison

On May 25th, The Bryce Is Right dealt Mookie Betts to Kirby's Dream Team in exchange for Randy Arozarena. Betts, playing shortstop for LAD, is the kind of player who contributes across every batting category on a weekly basis. Arozarena, out of SEA, brings speed and solid counting stats. The difference between them is real, and Kirby's Dream Team came out of this deal holding the better hand.

The case for The Bryce Is Right is an active roster philosophy — they added Peña and Walker from the wire the same week, clearly betting that depth and churn can replace a single elite player. That's a workable strategy in leagues with the right waiver wire environment. The risk is that Mookie Betts is precisely the kind of player who covers roster weaknesses without requiring constant management, and losing that safety net means the surrounding pieces have to perform every week.

What makes the timing strange is that Kirby's Dream Team received Betts and then immediately benched him for two days of production. Trading for an elite player and not deploying him is a theme worth watching — because if it becomes a pattern, the advantage this trade delivers starts to erode in a hurry.

The Numbers Don't Lie

by Gerald R. Pemberton III

Ep-Skenes Files led the league in three categories this week — innings pitched (74.1), strikeouts (81), and saves-plus-holds (10) — and tied Kirby's Dream Team for the lead in runs (41). They lost 7-5-0 to Professor McGonigle. The explanation is in the ERA column: 5.09, the worst mark in the league. Log enough innings at that clip and the quality categories — ERA, WHIP, K/BB — all swing the other direction. Volume without efficiency is just more damage.

McGonigle's staff did the opposite. A 2.04 ERA, 0.98 WHIP, and 4.75 K/BB in 57.1 innings — all three led the league. The offense was light: five home runs and 20 RBI, both the fewest in the league. None of that mattered. Winning three pitching categories clean tends to carry a matchup, and it did here.

Juiced Balls posted the strongest batting week in the league by several measures — first in HR (17), SB (10), and OBP (.410), and tied for the AVG lead at .304. Babe Ruthless hit .304 as well, to the exact thousandth, while leading the league in hits (73) and RBI (44). The matchup went 6-5-1 to Babe Ruthless. Juiced Balls held the better ERA (3.71 vs. 4.29) and WHIP (1.10 vs. 1.36); Ruthless countered with more strikeouts (54 vs. 49) and a better K/BB (3.60 vs. 2.88). Three batting categories won outright, a fourth shared, and Juiced Balls still finished on the wrong side of the ledger.

Risky Bettiness batted .211 this week — 55 points below the league average of .266 — and put up 39 hits against a league average of 61.8. Their K/BB of 4.33, meanwhile, ran well above the league average of 3.58. An offense that dormant drops batting categories faster than pitching command can recover them. Jr Jays won 8-4-0, and the hitting numbers account for most of those four lost categories.
